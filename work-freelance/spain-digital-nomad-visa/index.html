In short

  • Spain calls it the international teleworker visa (teletrabajo de carácter internacional). It is for non-EU nationals working remotely for companies based outside Spain.
  • Income: 200% of the Spanish minimum wage a month. With the 2026 minimum wage of €1,221 a month, that is €2,442 a month, plus €915.75 for one family member and €305.25 for each one after that.
  • You need a degree, vocational or business-school qualification, or at least three years of experience in similar work, and a work or client relationship of at least three months.
  • From abroad you apply for the visa (up to one year) at the Spanish consulate for your country of residence. Already legally in Spain, you can apply to the UGE for a residence permit of up to three years without a visa.
  • Employees may only work for the foreign company; freelancers may take Spanish clients for up to 20% of their work.

Spain's digital nomad visa, officially the international teleworker visa, lets a non-EU national live in Spain while working remotely for companies based abroad. You need income of 200% of the Spanish minimum wage, which on 31 October 2026 means €2,442 a month, plus a degree or three years of relevant experience. You apply at the Spanish consulate for your country of residence, or for a residence permit from inside Spain if you are there legally.

What the visa is, in Spain's own words

The legal name is "teletrabajo de carácter internacional". Article 74 bis of Ley 14/2013, added at the end of 2022 by the Startups Law, describes it as residence for a third-country national who works at a distance for companies located outside Spain, using only computer, telematic and telecommunication means.

Two limits follow from that wording. The job has to be one that can be done fully online: the Ministry of Inclusion's FAQ rules out roles that need someone on site for production, staff supervision or sales visits. And EU citizens, or anyone covered by EU law, cannot use it at all.

There is also a limit on who pays you:

Your relationship Who you can work for while in Spain
Employee of a foreign company Only companies based outside Spain
Freelancer with foreign clients Spanish companies too, up to 20% of your total professional activity

Source: Ley 14/2013, art. 74 bis, on boe.es, checked 31 October 2026.

If your employer has a branch in Spain and moves you there, the UGE says that is an intra-company transfer, a separate permit, and not this one.

For the general picture of remote-work visas and how Spain compares, see our guide to the digital nomad visa.

How much income you need in 2026

The rule is a percentage, not a fixed sum. The Moscow consulate page and the UGE FAQ word it the same way: the main applicant shows an amount that represents, every month, 200% of the Spanish minimum wage (SMI). A household of two adds 75% of the SMI, and each further member adds 25%.

The SMI for 2026 is set by Real Decreto 126/2026, published in the BOE on 19 February 2026: €1,221 a month, with an annual floor of €17,094, applying from 1 January to 31 December 2026. The amounts below are our arithmetic from that monthly figure.

Who Share of SMI Per month in 2026
Main applicant 200% €2,442
First family member 75% €915.75
Each further family member 25% €305.25
Applicant plus one family member 275% €3,357.75

These figures are gross. The UGE FAQ adds that the money has to come from income from work, which is why consulates ask for payslips, bank statements and the contract.

Watch the date on any document you read. NeedPro's checked fact for this visa points to the requirements sheet of the Spanish consulate in Santiago de Chile, and on 31 October 2026 that PDF still said "El SMI de 2023 es de 15.120 euros brutos anuales". The percentage in it is right; the euro figure is three years old. The SMI changes every year, so the next decree will change the amount again.

Degree or three years of experience

Article 74 bis accepts "qualified professionals" in one of two ways:

  • a degree or postgraduate degree from a recognised university, vocational training school or business school;
  • at least three years of professional experience.

The Santiago de Chile sheet explains what that looks like in the file. The degree has to be apostilled if it is not Spanish, or you show three years of experience in functions similar to the remote job. For regulated professions, such as medicine or law, the degree has to be formally recognised under Spanish rules. If you are unsure how your qualification is read abroad, our page on ENIC-NARIC centres explains who compares foreign degrees.

The work relationship and the company

Ley 14/2013, article 74 ter, sets three conditions on the job itself:

  1. The company, or group of companies, you work with has had real and continuous activity for at least one year.
  2. You can show the work can be done remotely: an employer's letter authorising remote work from Spain, or a contract setting out the terms for a freelancer.
  3. The relationship has lasted at least the last three months before you apply, as an employee or under a commercial contract.

Social security is part of the file. The UGE FAQ says registration in Spain is compulsory: an employer registers as a non-resident company and enrols you in the general scheme, unless a social security agreement between Spain and your country lets you keep your home cover with a certificate. Freelancers register as self-employed (RETA), and that cannot be replaced by a bilateral agreement.

Tax residence is a separate question, and this page does not cover it. Ask the Agencia Tributaria.

Route one: the visa at a consulate

If you live outside Spain, you apply for the visa at the Spanish consulate for your country of residence. The Moscow consulate states it in one line: "El interesado debe solicitar el visado ante el Consulado de España en la demarcación de su país de residencia." It accepts nationals and legal residents of its district; residents prove it with their residence permit or a valid visa. The UGE FAQ confirms you cannot apply for the residence permit from abroad.

What the consulate pages list, checked 31 October 2026:

  • National visa application form and photo
  • Passport valid for at least one year, with two blank pages, issued less than 10 years ago
  • Proof of residence in the consulate's district
  • Company certificate: length of your employment or contract, and permission to work remotely from Spain
  • Company registration certificate showing at least one year of activity
  • Proof of income at the 200% level
  • Criminal record certificate from every country you lived in over the last two years, plus a sworn statement covering five years
  • Health insurance from an insurer authorised in Spain, with no co-payments or waiting periods; travel insurance does not count
  • Degree or proof of three years of experience
  • Visa fee receipt

Foreign documents need an apostille or legalisation and a Spanish translation. Each consulate publishes its own list and fee, so take the list from the consulate that covers you. The passport rule is tighter than most countries ask; our page on passport validity covers the general rules.

The visa lasts up to one year, or for the work period if that is shorter, and on its own it lets you live and work remotely in Spain.

Route two: residence from inside Spain

Article 74 quinquies lets foreigners who are legally in Spain apply directly for the residence permit. The Santiago de Chile consulate sheet gives a regular tourist stay as an example of being there legally. You apply online to the Unidad de Grandes Empresas y Colectivos Estratégicos (UGE) of the Ministry of Inclusion, yourself or through an accredited representative.

Visa at a consulate Residence permit from the UGE
Where you are Abroad, in your country of residence Legally in Spain
Valid for Up to 1 year Up to 3 years
Renewal Switch to the permit 60 days before expiry Renewable for 2 years at a time
Decision time Set by the consulate 20 days, per the consulate pages

The UGE FAQ lists the fee for an initial application as €73.26, paid online before you file. Being "legally" in Spain is the key condition: the FAQ says you must not be in Spain irregularly.

Family members

A spouse or partner, minor children, adult children who still depend on you financially, and dependent parents can come with you or join later. The UGE FAQ says their permit lets them live and work in Spain without restrictions, as employees or self-employed. Each family member adds to the income requirement, as in the table above, and needs their own documents, including proof of the family link.

Before you apply

  1. Work out your figure: 200% of the current SMI a month, plus family members. Check that the year on the SMI is the current one.
  2. Make sure your work relationship is at least three months old and the company at least one year old.
  3. Find the Spanish consulate for the country where you live on exteriores.gob.es and download its own list.
  4. Start the criminal record certificate, apostille and translations early.
  5. If you are already legally in Spain, compare the UGE route.

The consulate or the UGE makes the decision, and we are not lawyers. If the rules for remote work differ from the job you have, compare a regular route such as the Turkey work permit, where the employer files. Questions people ask

How much income do I need for the Spain digital nomad visa in 2026?

200% of the Spanish minimum wage (SMI) a month. Real Decreto 126/2026 sets the 2026 SMI at €1,221 a month, so the main applicant needs €2,442 a month. A first family member adds 75% (€915.75) and each further one 25% (€305.25). The consulate decides what proof it accepts.

Can I apply for the Spanish digital nomad visa from inside Spain?

The visa itself is a consulate procedure. If you are legally in Spain, for example on a regular tourist stay, the law lets you apply to the UGE for the residence permit instead, without a prior visa. The consulates say the UGE decides within 20 days.

Where do I apply if I live outside my home country?

At the Spanish consulate that covers the country where you live. The Moscow consulate, for example, accepts nationals and legal residents of its district, and asks residents for their residence permit or valid visa as proof.

Do I need a university degree?

No, a degree is one of two ways in. Article 74 bis of Ley 14/2013 accepts graduates and postgraduates of recognised universities, vocational training or business schools, or at least three years of professional experience. Regulated professions need the degree recognised in Spain.

How long does the Spanish digital nomad visa last?

The visa lasts up to one year, or less if the work period is shorter. Within 60 days before it ends you can apply in Spain for the residence permit, which is valid for up to three years and renewable for two years at a time.
