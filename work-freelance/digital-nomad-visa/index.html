A digital nomad visa is a long-stay visa or residence permit for people who live in one country while working for an employer or clients based in another. It does not open the local job market. Each country sets its own bar: on 31 October 2026, Spain asked for 200% of its minimum wage, Thailand's DTV for 500,000 baht in savings, the UAE for a USD 3,500 monthly salary and Indonesia's E33G for USD 60,000 a year.

What a digital nomad visa actually is

There is no single "digital nomad visa" that works across borders. The phrase is a nickname for national programs that answer one question: may a foreigner live here while the paycheck comes from abroad?

The official names show how differently countries frame it. Spain calls it a residence visa for international telework. Thailand files its version, the Destination Thailand Visa, under tourism and soft power. The UAE issues a virtual work residence visa. Indonesia gives a remote worker visa with the code E33G.

What they share is the employer's address. Spain's consulates describe the visa as being for non-EU nationals working remotely "para empresas radicadas fuera del territorio nacional", for companies based outside Spain. Indonesia asks for an employment contract with a company established outside Indonesia. The UAE asks for proof of work outside the UAE.

Why a tourist stay is not the same thing

Most people searching for a nomad visa already work remotely, and many do it from a beach on a visitor stay. Whether that is allowed has no clean official answer. NeedPro's checked fact on this question says so directly: immigration law in most countries defines work by where the employer sits and by work permits, and does not regulate a foreign client with the worker physically present.

Only one country among the four writes a limit down. Indonesia's immigration portal lists the E33G conditions, and one of them reads: "You are prohibited from working by receiving compensation, wages, or any similar from individuals or companies in Indonesia." That applies to people who hold the remote worker visa.

A visitor stay also has a clock. In the Schengen area it is 90 days in any 180, counted across all member countries, and days on a national long-stay visa are left out of that count. Our Schengen calculator shows how many visitor days you have left.

The fact that Thailand, the UAE, Spain and Indonesia built separate visas for remote work suggests they treat it as needing its own status. Do not settle this from blogs. For a specific country, ask its immigration authority or a lawyer licensed there.

Thailand's DTV in two paragraphs

The DTV is the odd one out. It is a five-year multiple-entry visa with a stay of up to 180 days per entry, and the money test is savings, not salary: a bank statement showing at least 500,000 baht. The Thai embassy checklist we read asks for the last three months of statements.

Rules for applicants changed on 31 August 2026. The consulate in Fukuoka now asks for proof of permanent residence in the country where you apply and a criminal record certificate. Embassy fees differ by country. The full picture, including the extension and the work limits, is in our DTV visa Thailand guide.

What a nomad visa does not let you do

None of these visas is a work permit for the local market. The Thai embassy in Budapest describes the DTV as a special kind of tourist visa whose holders may not get a Thai work permit, work for companies in Thailand or freelance for Thai clients. Indonesia's ban on local pay was quoted above.

If a company in the country you live in wants to hire you, the route is a work permit, filed under that country's labor rules. Türkiye is a clear example: the employer applies to the Ministry of Labour before the first working day, and a tourist entry cannot be converted into an application. See how a Turkish work permit works.

Tax is a separate matter too. A residence visa does not settle where you are tax resident; check that with the tax authority of the country you move to and of your home country.

How to check a nomad visa before you apply

  1. Find the program's official name and the government site that issues it. Agency pages and nomad blogs often lag behind rule changes.
  2. Check which measure applies to you: monthly salary, yearly income, or savings held for a set period.
  3. Read where you may apply. Some missions now accept only nationals or residents of their own district.
  4. Check your employer's position. Most programs ask for a contract or certificate from a company registered abroad.
  5. Confirm whether local clients are allowed. For the DTV and the E33G they are not.
  6. Write down the date you read the rules. Fees and documents change, and the figure that counts is the one on the day you file.

The decision is made by the consulate or immigration service of each country. Confirm the rules on its official site before you file. Questions people ask

What is a digital nomad visa?

It is a visa or residence permit for someone who lives in a country while working remotely for an employer or clients based elsewhere. Each country names it differently: Spain's international teleworker visa, Thailand's Destination Thailand Visa (DTV), the UAE's virtual work visa, Indonesia's E33G remote worker visa.

Can I work remotely on a tourist visa?

There is no clean official yes. Most immigration laws do not address a foreign client with the worker physically present, and the countries that created nomad visas treat remote work as needing its own status. Indonesia states a ban on pay from Indonesian people or companies even for E33G holders. Ask the immigration authority of the country you are in.

Which country has the lowest income requirement for a digital nomad visa?

The four we checked use different measures, so a straight ranking misleads. The UAE asks for a USD 3,500 monthly salary, Spain for 200% of its minimum wage a month, Indonesia for USD 60,000 a year, and Thailand for a 500,000 baht bank balance rather than an income.

Does a digital nomad visa let me work for a local company?

No. These visas are built around work for a company outside the country. The Thai embassy in Budapest says DTV holders may not get a Thai work permit or freelance for Thai clients, and Indonesia bans pay from Indonesian sources on the E33G. A local job needs a work permit.

Do I pay tax in the country of my nomad visa?

Tax residence is a separate question from the visa. Check it with the tax authority of the country you move to and of your home country.
