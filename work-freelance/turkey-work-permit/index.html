In short

  • A foreigner must hold a work permit or a work permit exemption before starting work in Türkiye.
  • The employer files the application with the Ministry of Labour and Social Security, through e-Devlet.
  • From abroad, you apply for a work visa at the Turkish mission in your country of nationality and get a 16-digit reference number for the employer.
  • From inside Türkiye, the route is open only if you hold a residence permit of at least 6 months that is still valid. A tourist stay does not qualify.
  • The ministry decides within 30 days of a complete file. A first permit is valid for up to one year.

To work in Türkiye you need a work permit, or a work permit exemption, before your first day. The employer files the application with the Ministry of Labour and Social Security. You reach it one of two ways: from abroad, through a work visa application at the Turkish mission in your country, or from inside Türkiye if you already hold a residence permit of at least six months. That was the rule on 30 October 2026.

Do you need a work permit to work in Türkiye?

Yes. The ministry's FAQ on work permits opens with the rule, in Turkish: a foreigner covered by the law must obtain a work permit or a work permit exemption "Türkiye'de çalışmaya başlamadan önce", before starting work in Türkiye.

Exemptions are not automatic either. Some groups hold them, and the ministry lists examples such as holders of the Turkish Blue Card (Mavi Kart), refugees and members of diplomatic missions. An exemption still has to be obtained in advance through the exemption system.

What happens without a permit is spelled out in the same FAQ: foreigners found working without a permit and their employers both face administrative fines, and the worker is referred to the Ministry of the Interior for deportation proceedings.

Who applies: you or the employer?

The employer. A work permit in Türkiye is tied to a job, and the application is filed by the employer online, through e-Devlet and the ministry's e-İzin system. What you do yourself depends on where you are when the process starts. The ministry's page on application types lists two main routes.

Route Where you are What you need first What happens next
From abroad (yurt dışı) In your country of nationality A work visa application at the Turkish embassy or consulate You get a 16-digit reference number; the employer files with it
From inside (yurt içi) In Türkiye A valid residence permit issued for at least 6 months The employer files online; you hold a foreign ID number starting with 99

Source: csgb.gov.tr, application types and FAQ, checked 30 October 2026.

The page also lists separate procedures for holders of temporary protection and international protection identity cards, and the Turquoise Card has its own procedure.

Applying from abroad

If you have no Turkish residence permit, the ministry says you go to the Turkish diplomatic mission in the country of your nationality and apply for a work visa ("çalışma vizesi"). The mission gives you a 16-digit reference number ("16 haneli referans numarası").

You pass that number to the employer, and the employer files the work permit application online using it. The two halves have to meet: your visa application at the consulate and the employer's application with the ministry.

Ask the mission what documents it wants with the work visa application. They are set by the mission, and we do not list them here.

Applying from inside Türkiye

The domestic route exists only for foreigners who are already in Türkiye with a valid residence permit issued for at least six months ("en az altı (6) ay süreli geçerli ikamet izni"). The ministry adds that such a foreigner already has a Turkish foreign identification number starting with 99.

A tourist stay does not meet this condition. A visa-free entry, a tourist visa or an e-Visa is not a residence permit, so it does not open the domestic route, and NeedPro's checked fact says it directly: a tourist or e-Visa entry gives no right to work. If you are in Türkiye as a visitor and get a job offer, the route is the work visa at the mission in your own country.

If you are applying for a residence permit first, health insurance is part of that file: see health insurance for a Turkish residence permit.

How long it takes and how long it lasts

The ministry's FAQ gives these figures:

Item Rule (csgb.gov.tr, checked 30 October 2026)
Decision time Within 30 days of a complete application, counted from submission or from the upload of requested documents
First permit (tied to one employer) Valid for up to one year
First extension Up to two years
Later extensions Up to three years
When to apply for an extension From 60 days before the permit expires, and before it expires
Working while an extension is pending Up to 90 days, if the job and workplace stay the same
Appeal against a refusal Within 30 days of notification; then administrative court

A late extension is treated as a first application, according to the application types page. Mark the date 60 days before expiry.

There are other kinds of permit too. An indefinite permit is open to foreigners with a long-term residence permit or at least eight years of legal work permits. An independent permit lets a foreigner work on their own account.

Work permit and residence permit: how they connect

The ministry says a valid work permit or exemption counts as a residence permit. The reverse is not true: a residence permit on its own does not give the right to work, except for refugees and holders of subsidiary protection status.

So someone living in Türkiye on a residence permit for another purpose still needs a work permit before taking a job. The residence permit only decides which route the employer can use.

Two practical points come with moving to Türkiye for work. Türkiye counts passport validity from the end of your permitted stay, not from entry: see passport validity rules. And a phone bought abroad has to be registered: see how IMEI registration works in Türkiye.

Steps in order

  1. Get a written job offer from an employer in Türkiye.
  2. If you are abroad, apply for a work visa at the Turkish mission in your country of nationality and keep the 16-digit reference number.
  3. If you are in Türkiye, check that your residence permit was issued for at least six months and is still valid.
  4. Give the employer your reference number or your foreign ID number starting with 99.
  5. Wait for the ministry's decision before starting work.
  6. Diary the date 60 days before the permit expires for the extension.

Other countries draw the same line in their own law. China, for example, needs both a work permit and a work-type residence permit: see China's work permit rules.

Working remotely for an employer abroad is a different case, and some countries have a separate visa for it: see our overview of the digital nomad visa, and the details for Thailand's DTV and the Spain digital nomad visa.

The ministry decides each application, and the consulate decides the work visa. Confirm the procedure on csgb.gov.tr or with the Turkish mission in your country. Questions people ask

Do I need a work permit to work in Turkey?

Yes, if you are a foreigner covered by Turkish labour migration law. The Ministry of Labour and Social Security says you must obtain a work permit or a work permit exemption before you start working. Working without one leads to administrative fines for you and the employer.

Can I apply for a Turkish work permit on a tourist visa or e-Visa?

No. From inside Türkiye the ministry accepts applications only for foreigners holding a residence permit valid for at least six months. Without it, you apply for a work visa at the Turkish mission in your country of nationality.

Who applies for the work permit, me or the employer?

The employer files the application online. If you are abroad, you first apply for a work visa at the Turkish consulate or embassy and get a 16-digit reference number; the employer then files using that number.

How long does a Turkish work permit take?

The ministry's FAQ says a complete application is decided within thirty days. The clock runs from submission, or from the day any missing documents are uploaded.

Does a Turkish residence permit let me work?

No. The ministry says a residence permit alone does not give the right to work, except for refugees and holders of subsidiary protection. A work permit, on the other hand, also counts as a residence permit.
