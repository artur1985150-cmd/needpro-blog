In short

  • The Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) is valid for 5 years with multiple entries and allows up to 180 days per entry.
  • Each stay can be extended once at Thai immigration, for up to another 180 days.
  • You need at least 500,000 baht in the bank, shown on statements; most missions ask for three months of them.
  • Since 31 August 2026 applicants must prove permanent residence and submit a criminal record certificate.
  • Thai embassies call the DTV a special kind of tourist visa: no Thai work permit, no work for Thai companies or Thai clients.

The Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) is a 5-year multiple-entry visa that allows up to 180 days in Thailand per entry, and each stay can be extended once by up to 180 days. It is for remote workers and freelancers paid from abroad, people on Thai soft power courses such as Muay Thai, and their families. You need at least 500,000 baht in the bank and, since 31 August 2026, proof of permanent residence where you apply.

What the DTV is

Thailand announced the DTV in May 2024 as a new visa type: a stay of up to 180 days per entry, 5 years of validity, multiple entries. Thai missions still publish the same core terms. The consulate in Fukuoka, in a page updated on 7 September 2026, lists "Multiple Entries (No limit of entries as long as the visa is still valid)", a validity of 5 years from the date of issue and a stay of 180 days each time.

The visa is not a long-stay residence permit, and the missions say so. The embassy in Budapest calls it "a special kind of tourist visa" and adds that it "is not intended for long-term relocation". If you need a longer view of Thailand's entry options, start with the Thailand visa exemption rules, which changed in September 2026.

Who can apply

Thai missions list three groups:

Track Who Proof of purpose (examples from mission pages)
Workcation Digital nomads, remote workers, foreign talent, freelancers Employment contract or certificate from a foreign company, company registration, professional portfolio
Thai soft power Muay Thai, Thai cooking, sport training, medical treatment, seminars, music festivals Letter of acceptance from the school, camp or organizer; appointment letter from a hospital
Family Spouse and children under 20 of a DTV holder Proof of relationship and the main holder's passport and DTV approval

Sources: MFA checklist (Kuala Lumpur), Budapest, Fukuoka and Singapore mission pages, checked 31 October 2026.

Missions add their own detail. Budapest says a soft power activity should last at least 6 months. Singapore issues the soft power DTV only to Singapore citizens and permanent residents.

How much money you need

The figure on every mission page is 500,000 baht. How you prove it varies. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs checklist used by the embassy in Kuala Lumpur asks for a bank statement for the last 3 months with an ending balance of no less than 500,000 baht, plus salary slips for the last 6 months. Fukuoka wants a minimum balance of 500,000 baht in each of the last three months. Budapest asks for a 6-month bank statement and says crypto and other investment statements are not accepted.

This is separate from the funds check at the border. Thai immigration may ask any arriving foreigner to show money for the stay. For a tourist visa or a non-immigrant visa the Royal Thai Government lists 20,000 baht per person or 40,000 baht per family, with children under 12 exempt. The officer at the checkpoint decides on entry.

The rule change of 31 August 2026

On 28 August 2026 Thailand's Department of Consular Affairs announced new supporting documents for the DTV. The embassy in Moscow summarized it in English:

  1. A document showing your current location is replaced by proof of permanent residence.
  2. A certificate of criminal record clearance is added.

The rule took effect at 00.00 on 31 August 2026, Thai time. Applications submitted in the e-Visa system before that date are processed under the old list. The consulate in Osaka words the first point more tightly: proof of permanent residence "in the country/territory where the visa application is submitted", and a criminal record certificate from your country of nationality or permanent residence.

In practice this means you apply where you live long term, not where you happen to be. Ask the mission that covers your country what it accepts as proof of residence.

How long you can stay, and the extension

Each entry gives up to 180 days. The embassy in Singapore says the stay "can be renewed at the Thai Immigration Police Office for another 180 days". The embassy in Ankara gives the same rule: one extension per entry, for no more than 180 days. You apply for it in Thailand, at the immigration office, while the current stay is still running.

Where you sleep matters for that application. Thai law makes the owner of the home or the hotel report a foreigner's address to immigration, and a missing report can hold up an extension. See what TM30 is and who files it.

Can you work on a DTV?

You can keep working for an employer or clients outside Thailand: that is what the workcation track is for. You cannot take Thai work. The embassy in Budapest is explicit that holders "are prohibited from obtaining a Thai work permit and working for companies in Thailand or performing freelance work for Thai clients."

Thailand's government portal lists seven business activities that need no work permit, such as attending meetings, seminars and trade shows, holding business negotiations and attending your own company's board meetings. These cover attendance and talks, not delivering work for a client in Thailand. Urgent work finished within 15 days needs no work permit either, but it must be notified to the registrar on form TM.10.

A job with a Thai company needs a work permit, not a DTV. Other countries draw the same line; Türkiye's version, where the employer files before the first working day, is in our Turkish work permit guide. For how the DTV compares with Spain, the UAE and Indonesia, see digital nomad visas side by side.

Tax residence is a separate question from the visa. Check it with the Thai Revenue Department and the tax authority of your home country.

Applying, step by step

  1. Find the Thai embassy or consulate that covers the country where you are a permanent resident, and read its DTV page.
  2. Gather proof of permanent residence and a criminal record certificate, as required since 31 August 2026.
  3. Prepare bank statements showing at least 500,000 baht for the period your mission asks for.
  4. Add proof of your track: a foreign employment contract and portfolio, a course or treatment letter, or proof of family ties.
  5. Apply and pay through the official e-Visa system at thaievisa.go.th. Fees are set by each mission: on 31 October 2026 Budapest listed 350 euros, Singapore 500 Singapore dollars and Fukuoka 52,000 yen.
  6. Keep the approval with your passport. Inside Thailand, apply for any extension at immigration while your stay is still valid.

The embassy or consulate decides the visa, and the officer at the border decides entry. Confirm the current list on the page of your mission and on thaievisa.go.th before you file. Questions people ask

How long can I stay in Thailand on a DTV?

Up to 180 days per entry. The Thai embassy in Singapore says each stay can be renewed once at the immigration office for another 180 days. The visa itself is valid for 5 years with multiple entries.

How much money do I need for the DTV?

At least 500,000 baht, shown on bank statements. Missions word it differently: the Kuala Lumpur checklist asks for three months of statements with an ending balance of 500,000 baht, Fukuoka for a minimum balance of 500,000 baht in each of the last three months.

Can I work for Thai clients on a DTV?

No. The embassy in Budapest says DTV holders are prohibited from obtaining a Thai work permit, working for companies in Thailand or doing freelance work for Thai clients. The workcation track covers work for employers and clients abroad.

What changed for the DTV on 31 August 2026?

Thailand's Department of Consular Affairs announced on 28 August 2026 that applicants must submit proof of permanent residence instead of a document showing their current location, plus a criminal record certificate. Applications filed in the e-Visa system before 31 August are handled under the old list.

How much does the DTV cost?

The fee is set in local currency by each mission. On 31 October 2026 Budapest listed 350 euros, Singapore 500 Singapore dollars and Fukuoka 52,000 yen. Check the fee on the page of the mission that covers where you live.
