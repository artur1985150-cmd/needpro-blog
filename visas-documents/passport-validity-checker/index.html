In short

  • The checker answers one question: on the dates of your trip, does your passport meet the destination's validity rule?
  • It covers 7 destinations whose rule we read on the official page: the Schengen area, Turkey, the UAE, India, Indonesia, the UK and the US.
  • Each country counts from a different day: the day you enter, the day you leave or the day you apply.
  • For the Schengen area it also checks the issue date: a passport more than 10 years old fails even if it has not expired.
  • It checks dates only. Your nationality, your visa and your airline can add conditions.

A passport can be in date and still not be valid for a trip, because each country wants it to last some time past a day of its own choosing. Pick your destination below and enter your passport and trip dates. The checker shows the exact date your passport has to be valid until and how many days you are over or short. How the checker counts

It takes the destination's rule from the table above and does three things.

  1. It finds the day the country counts from. For the UAE and Indonesia that is the day you enter. For the Schengen area, Turkey, the UK and the US it is the day you leave. India counts from the day you apply for the visa.
  2. It adds the time in the rule: 3 months, 6 months, 60 days, or nothing at all for the UK.
  3. It compares the result with your expiry date and tells you the gap in days.

A month here is a calendar month. Leave the Schengen area on 10 January and the passport has to be valid on 10 April. Leave on 30 November and the date is 28 February, or 29 February in a leap year. If your result is within a few days of the line, treat it as a fail: an officer or an airline agent may count more strictly than we do.

Why seven destinations and not two hundred? Those are the ones whose rule we could read on the border authority's own page. Each row links to that page and carries the date we opened it. The longer explanation of every rule is in passport validity requirements by country.

Why the same passport passes in one country and fails in another

Take a passport that expires on 1 June 2027 and a ten-day trip from 1 to 10 January 2027.

  • UK: fine. GOV.UK asks for a passport "valid for the whole of your stay", and 1 June is long after 10 January.
  • Schengen area: fine on the expiry date. Three months after 10 January is 10 April.
  • UAE: not enough. Six months from 1 January is 1 July, and the passport ends a month earlier.
  • Turkey: fine. The stay plus 60 days ends on 11 March.

None of these four answers contradicts the others: each country simply asks its own question. Remember only "the six-month rule" and you may cancel a trip the passport was fine for. Remember only "three months" and you can be stopped on the way to Dubai.

The Schengen issue date

The Schengen rule has a second half that has nothing to do with expiry. The EU's Your Europe page says the passport "must have been issued within the last 10 years". So a passport that is valid for more than ten years on paper fails on the day it turns ten, whatever the expiry date says.

That is why the form asks for the issue date. The checker compares it with the day you enter. For every other destination you can leave the field empty.

The dates of the trip have their own limit in the Schengen area, 90 days in any 180. The Schengen calculator counts those.

The US gives two answers

CBP's rule for visitors is six months beyond the intended stay, with an exception: citizens of the countries on its list "need only have a passport valid for their intended period of stay". The list we read on 13 November 2026 is dated 18 December 2025 and is long. The checker cannot know your citizenship, so for the US it prints both dates. Open the CBP page from the result, find your country, and use the line that applies to you.

A US trip also depends on an ESTA or a visa, and an ESTA ends with the passport it was issued for. See how long an ESTA is valid. The UK works the same way with its ETA: what a UK ETA costs and who needs one.

What the checker does not know

  • Your nationality. Some countries set different rules by passport, and bilateral agreements exist. The seven rules here are the general ones the official page gives.
  • Your visa. A visa can be shorter than the passport and can ask for more: India's e-Visa page also wants two blank pages for stamps.
  • Your airline. GOV.UK warns that some airlines apply stricter rules than the border they fly to.
  • The state of the passport. A damaged or reported-lost passport is not valid on any date.
  • Indonesia's rule today. Its e-VOA portal did not open for us on 30 October or on 13 November 2026, so that row shows 4 September 2026, the day NeedPro last read it. Check the portal yourself before a trip to Bali; the visa itself is covered in Bali visa on arrival.

If your passport falls short

A fail here is cheap: you found out at home. The options, in the order people usually take them:

  1. Renew before you book. Processing times are in how long it takes to get a passport.
  2. If the trip is already booked, check whether moving the dates by a few days changes the answer. For rules counted from the day you leave, a shorter stay can be enough.
  3. Ask the consulate of the destination. It can tell you whether an exception exists for your nationality. We cannot, and a checker cannot either.

We are not lawyers, and nothing on this page is a promise that you will be let in. The rules are quoted from the official pages listed under the article, with the dates we read them. The decision at the border belongs to the officer.

Questions people ask

How do I check if my passport is valid for travel?

Compare two dates in the passport, issue and expiry, with the rule of the country you are going to. The checker on this page does it for 7 destinations: you pick one, enter your passport and trip dates, and it shows the date the passport must be valid until.

Does this checker tell me whether my passport number is valid or my application is approved?

No. It only compares dates with a destination's published rule. Whether a passport has been cancelled or an application approved is a question for the authority that issued it.

My passport expires in 4 months. Can I go to Dubai?

No, by the UAE government portal's rule: passports must be valid for at least 6 months from the date of entry. With 4 months left the checker shows how many days short you are.

Why does the checker ask for the issue date?

For the Schengen area. The EU's Your Europe page says the passport must have been issued within the last 10 years, as well as being valid 3 months after you leave. The other six rules do not use the issue date, so you can leave it empty for them.

Is anything I type sent or stored?

No. The dates are compared in your browser by a small script on this page. Nothing is sent to us and nothing is saved, so the form is empty again when you reload.
