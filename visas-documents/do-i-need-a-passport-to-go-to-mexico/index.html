Do I need a passport to go to Mexico? Air vs land rules

In short

  • Flying to Mexico: bring a US passport book. Mexico's immigration institute (INM) says the passport card cannot be used for international air trips.
  • Driving or walking in: INM accepts a passport or a passport card, but the card only works at land crossings and for visiting the border zone.
  • Coming back by land or sea, CBP accepts a passport, passport card, Enhanced Driver's License or a NEXUS, SENTRI or FAST card. By air, children need their own passport too.
  • Back home after less than 48 hours, your duty-free allowance drops from $800 to $200, and family members cannot combine their $200.
  • Not a US citizen? Mexico's rules depend on your nationality; some passports need a Mexican visa.

If you fly, yes. A US citizen flying to Mexico needs a US passport book, because Mexico's immigration institute says the passport card is not valid for international air trips. If you drive or walk across, the cheaper passport card works for land crossings and the border zone, and the US side accepts it when you come back. Travelers who are not US citizens follow rules that depend on their nationality.

Flying to Mexico: why the passport book

Two governments check you on this trip, and both have to be satisfied.

Mexico's side is set by the Instituto Nacional de Migración (INM). Its Multiple Immigration Form page, read on November 6, 2026, says a traveler must hold "a valid and current passport or card passport." Then it narrows the card: it "can only be used in the border crossings by land and for visiting the border zone; it might not be used to travel to the territory of the Mexican Republic or used for international air trips."

The US side is CBP. On its Western Hemisphere Travel Initiative page, checked the same day, the documents US citizens can show when returning by air are a US passport, a trusted traveler card (NEXUS, SENTRI or FAST), a military ID on official orders, or a Merchant Mariner document on official business. CBP adds: "children are also required to present their own passport when traveling by air."

Put together: a passport book covers both checks for an air trip, and the card covers neither.

Driving or walking across the border

By land, Mexico accepts the passport card, but only for the border crossing itself and the border zone. INM's wording, quoted above, rules it out for travel further into "the territory of the Mexican Republic." If you plan to drive beyond the border area, bring the passport book.

Entering by land, you also deal with the Forma Migratoria Múltiple (FMM), Mexico's visitor form. INM says you can get it online, that it gives you visitor status "without permission to work," and that it has "a maximum validity of 180 calendar days" for one entry only. The fee is paid through INM's service portal. INM also says that paying the fee "is not a guarantee to enter Mexico"; the officer at the crossing decides.

CBP describes the Enhanced Driver's License as a "convenient alternative for entering the United States from Canada, Mexico or the Caribbean through a land or sea port of entry." That is the US re-entry only. INM's page does not mention it.

Cruises to Mexico

Many Mexico cruises leave from and return to the same US port. CBP calls these closed-loop cruises and says US citizens on them "are able to enter the United States with a birth certificate and government-issued photo ID."

The same paragraph adds: "you may still be required to present a passport to enter the countries your cruise ship is visiting. Check with your cruise line." So the birth certificate solves the return to the US, not necessarily the day ashore in Mexico. Ask the cruise line in writing what it needs, and take the passport if you have one.

Traveling with children

For the US re-entry, CBP's rules split by transport:

  1. By air, every child needs their own passport.
  2. By land or sea, US and Canadian children under 16 "need only present a birth certificate or other proof of citizenship." The limit rises to under 19 for children traveling with a school, religious group or other youth group.
  3. CBP says the birth certificate "can be original, photocopy, or certified copy."

These are US rules for coming home. Mexico can set its own conditions for minors entering or leaving. Check with INM or a Mexican consulate before traveling with a child, especially with only one parent.

Coming back: duty-free limits and cash

CBP's "Types of Exemptions" page, checked November 6, 2026, sets the usual allowance at $800 of goods "as long as you bring them with you." Short trips are where people get caught out. The allowance falls to $200 if "you have not been out of the country for at least 48 hours" or "you have been out of the country more than once in a 30-day period."

A few details from the $200 rule matter for a weekend in Tijuana or Nogales:

  • Within the $200 you may include 50 cigarettes, 10 cigars and 150 milliliters (5 fl. oz.) of alcoholic drinks or perfume containing alcohol.
  • Go over $200 and "the entire amount will be dutiable," not just the excess.
  • Family members "may not combine their individual $200 exemptions."

Cash has its own rule. CBP's money page says federal law requires you to report amounts over $10,000 when entering or leaving the US, on FinCEN Form 105, and for families or groups the threshold applies to the total they carry together. Filing online is the route CBP recommends. For how cash rules work at the other end of a trip, see our guide to carrying cash into Europe.

Not a US citizen?

Then the answer depends on your passport. INM says that "in case the country of citizenship requires it, the passport must have a valid unexpired visa." Some nationalities enter Mexico on a passport alone and some need a Mexican visa; Mexico's consulates decide that, not us.

If you live in the US as a lawful permanent resident, CBP says WHTI does not change your rules for coming back: you present your permanent resident card (Form I-551). You still need whatever Mexico asks of your nationality to get in. Visitors from Visa Waiver countries who go from the US to Mexico and back also need to keep their US paperwork in order; our article on how long an ESTA is valid explains the two-year approval and the 90-day stay.

Before you book a Mexico trip

  1. Decide how you are traveling. Any flight leg means a passport book for everyone, children included.
  2. Check that the passport is valid for the whole trip; our page on passport validity rules by country explains how different countries count it.
  3. Driving past the border zone? Take the book, not the card.
  4. Crossing by land, sort out the FMM on INM's site before you go.
  5. On a cruise, ask the line which documents each port needs.
  6. Keep receipts for what you buy and count against $800, or $200 for short trips.
  7. Read the US Embassy's travel advisory. On November 6, 2026, the US Embassy and Consulates in Mexico showed Mexico at "Level 2: Exercise increased caution."

CBP's general advice is short: a US citizen traveling abroad "must comply with the document requirements for that country." For Mexico that means INM's rules, which can change, so look at INM's page again before you go. Questions people ask

Can I fly to Mexico with a passport card?

No. On its Multiple Immigration Form page, Mexico's National Institute of Migration (INM) says the passport card can only be used at land border crossings and for visiting the border zone, and not for international air trips.

Can I go to Mexico with just a driver's license or birth certificate?

INM lists a passport or a passport card for entering Mexico. A birth certificate counts only for coming back into the US on a closed-loop cruise (with photo ID) or for children under 16 crossing by land or sea, according to CBP.

Do children need a passport to go to Mexico?

By air, CBP says children must present their own passport to re-enter the US. By land or sea, US children under 16 (under 19 with a school, religious or youth group) can re-enter with a birth certificate. Check Mexico's own rules for minors with INM or a Mexican consulate.

Do I need a passport for a cruise to Mexico?

On a cruise that starts and ends at the same US port, CBP lets US citizens re-enter with a birth certificate and government-issued photo ID. CBP warns you may still need a passport for the countries the ship visits, so check with the cruise line.

How much can I bring back from Mexico duty free?

CBP's normal exemption is $800 of goods you carry with you. It drops to $200 if you were out of the US less than 48 hours or have already left more than once in 30 days.
