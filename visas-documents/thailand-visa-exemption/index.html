In short

  • The 60-day visa exemption ended on 15 September 2026. Entries from that date get up to 30 days.
  • It covers ordinary passports of 60 listed countries and territories, for tourism only.
  • At the border you must show a confirmed ticket out of Thailand within 30 days of arrival, or get a visa before you travel.
  • Land-border entries under the exemption are limited to twice per calendar year, with exceptions for Malaysia, Brunei, Indonesia and Singapore.
  • Every non-Thai traveller files the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) online, 3 days before arrival.

Thailand's tourist visa exemption now gives a stay of up to 30 days, not 60. The change applies to entries from 15 September 2026, for ordinary passports of 60 listed countries and territories, tourism only. At the border you need a confirmed ticket out within 30 days, and before you fly every non-Thai traveller files the TDAC arrival card online, 3 days ahead.

What changed on 15 September 2026

Many pages that rank for "Thailand visa exemption" still describe a 60-day stay. That rule is gone. On 1 September 2026 the Tourism Authority of Thailand wrote that the new framework "will replace Thailand's 60-day visa exemption measure, which will end on the same date", 15 September 2026.

The Royal Thai Embassy in Oslo, which NeedPro uses as the source for this rule, now says visitors are "eligible to stay in the country up to 30 days (starting from 15 September 2026)". We read the page on 30 October 2026.

If an airport page, a hotel or a forum post tells you 60 days, check its date. Anything written before September 2026 describes the old scheme.

Which countries qualify?

The Oslo embassy page lists 60 countries and territories, under the heading of ordinary passports that can visit Thailand "with 30 Days Visa Exemption for Tourist Purposes". Two conditions from the same page are easy to miss:

  • the scheme only covers passports issued by the listed countries;
  • "Travel documents for foreigners issued by these countries are not acceptable", so a refugee or stateless travel document from a listed country does not qualify.

We do not copy the list here because it changes and a copy goes stale. Open the embassy page for your country, find your nationality, and check the date at the top. If you are not on the list, you need a visa or another arrangement before you travel. If you hold an unusual passport or two nationalities, you can send them to the NeedPro bot, which answers from its checked facts with the source and check date.

Proof of onward travel

The embassy says you must show "proof of planned travel (confirmed air, train, bus, or boat tickets) to leave Thailand within 30 days of the arrival date". The next line: "Otherwise, a visa must be obtained before entering Thailand."

So a one-way ticket to Bangkok with a vague plan to "move on later" does not meet the rule. The exit ticket has to be confirmed and dated within 30 days of the day you land. A ticket for day 35 fails too.

Your passport is checked at the same counter. Thailand's own validity rule is not on the pages we used, so check it with the embassy; our passport validity guide explains how other countries count it.

Is there a limit on how often you can use it?

Yes, but the wording depends on where you read it.

The Tourism Authority of Thailand says that travellers on the 30-day exemption may enter at land-border checkpoints "no more than twice per calendar year". The limit does not apply to nationals of Malaysia, Brunei Darussalam, Indonesia and Singapore, or to other nationalities the Minister designates.

The Oslo embassy page is stricter for its own readers: Norwegian and Icelandic passport holders may use the exemption a maximum of twice in a calendar year "by all means (air/land/sea)".

Embassies publish rules for the nationalities they serve, so read the page of the Thai embassy in your own country. Neither page we used says whether a 30-day stay can be extended; ask the Immigration Bureau if you need longer.

The TDAC arrival card

Separate from the exemption, and needed whatever your passport or visa. The Immigration Bureau's guide says: "All non-Thai nationals entering the Kingdom of Thailand are required to complete the Thailand Digital Arrival Card online prior to entry." It replaces the old paper arrival card.

How it works, according to the same guide:

  1. Go to the official site, tdac.immigration.go.th, and fill in the form 3 days in advance of your arrival date.
  2. Download the card after you submit it. You can also download or print a copy from the email address you gave. Keep it for the whole stay.
  3. Read the result in the email. If it says "AUTOMATED BORDER CONTROL - ELIGIBLE", you can use the automatic gates; "IMMIGRATION COUNTER - MANUAL PROCESSING" means the regular counter.

The guide also says plainly that the TDAC "is not a visa". Filing it does not give you the right to enter. Other countries have their own online steps before a visa-free trip, with different rules: the UK has the ETA and the US has the ESTA.

After you arrive: where you stay matters too

Thailand also has a separate address notification for foreigners, the TM30. It is not the TDAC and not an arrival card. If you will not stay in a hotel, read what TM30 is and who files it before you go.

Checklist before you fly

  1. Check that your nationality is on the current list at the Thai embassy for your country.
  2. Make sure you travel on an ordinary passport issued by that country.
  3. Book and confirm a ticket out of Thailand dated within 30 days of arrival.
  4. If you plan to cross by land more than twice this year, read the embassy rules for your nationality first.
  5. File the TDAC 3 days before you land, and keep the downloaded card.
  6. If your trip is for work, study or longer than 30 days, apply for the right visa instead.

Entry is decided by the immigration officer, and lists and limits change. Confirm your case on the Thai embassy site for your country or with the Immigration Bureau.

Questions people ask

Is the Thailand 60 day visa exemption still available?

No. The Tourism Authority of Thailand said the 60-day measure would end on 15 September 2026, and the Royal Thai Embassy in Oslo lists a stay of up to 30 days starting from that date.

Which countries are on the Thailand visa exemption list?

The Royal Thai Embassy in Oslo listed 60 countries and territories on 30 October 2026. The list is for ordinary passports only and changes, so check your nationality on the current embassy page before booking.

Can I work or study on the visa exemption?

No. The embassy page says the scheme is for tourism purposes only. Work or study needs the matching visa.

How many times a year can I use the exemption?

For land-border checkpoints, the Tourism Authority of Thailand says no more than twice per calendar year, except for nationals of Malaysia, Brunei, Indonesia and Singapore. The Oslo embassy applies the twice-a-year limit to Norwegian and Icelandic passports for all means of travel. Check the embassy page for your country.

When do I fill in the TDAC?

The Immigration Bureau's guide says to submit it 3 days in advance of your arrival date. It is required for all non-Thai nationals and is not a visa.
