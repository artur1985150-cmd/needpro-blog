In short

  • There is no single 6-month rule. Each country counts from a different day: entry, departure, or the end of your permitted stay.
  • Schengen: valid 3 months after you leave the area, and issued within the last 10 years. The issue date catches people out.
  • UAE and Indonesia count 6 months from the day you enter; India counts 6 months from the day you apply for the visa.
  • Turkey: the passport must outlast your permitted stay by at least 60 days, so a 90-day stay needs 150 days.
  • UK: valid for the whole stay. An ETA or ESTA also ends when the passport it is linked to expires.

How long your passport must be valid depends on the country, and each one counts from a different day. On 30 October 2026 the Schengen area asked for 3 months after you leave plus an issue date within the last 10 years; the UAE and Indonesia asked for 6 months from entry; India for 6 months on the day you apply; Turkey for your permitted stay plus 60 days; the UK for the whole stay.

Why "6 months" is the wrong question

Most tables online say "3 months" or "6 months" and stop there. The number matters less than the day it is counted from. Three starting points show up in the official texts:

  • the day you arrive (UAE, Indonesia);
  • the day you leave (the Schengen area), or the last day you are allowed to stay (Turkey);
  • the day you apply for the visa (India).

A passport with 7 months left can pass in the UAE and still fail in Turkey if you plan a long stay. A passport with 2 years left can fail in the Schengen area because of its issue date. So check two dates in your passport, the date of issue and the date of expiry, against the rule of the country you are going to.

Schengen: check the issue date as well

The EU's Your Europe page puts two conditions in one sentence: your passport "should be valid for at least 3 months after the date you intend to leave the EU and it must have been issued within the last 10 years". Both have to hold at once.

The second condition is the one people miss. If your passport is still in date but its issue date is more than ten years before your trip, it fails the Schengen test anyway. Look at the issue date, not only the expiry date, before you book. The exit date you count from has a limit of its own, 90 days in any 180; the Schengen calculator works it out from your trip dates.

The same page says border officials may ask for supporting documents on arrival as well as the passport: an invitation letter, proof of lodging, a return or round-trip ticket. These rules are for non-EU nationals; EU, EEA and Swiss citizens travel under free-movement rules.

Turkey: your stay plus 60 days

Turkey's Presidency of Migration Management states it in its visa FAQ, in Turkish: you need a passport or travel document valid at least 60 days longer than the period you will stay ("Kalacağınız süreden en az 60 gün daha uzun süreli pasaport").

This is why people search for a "6 month passport rule Turkey" and find conflicting answers. The rule is tied to the length of your permitted stay. Work it out like this:

  1. Find how long your visa, visa exemption or residence permit lets you stay.
  2. Add 60 days.
  3. Count that many days from your entry date. Your passport must still be valid on that day.

For a 90-day stay, that is 150 days from entry. A shorter permitted stay needs less. The rule applies whether or not you need a visa. For Pakistani passport holders, who need a visa and can use the e-Visa only with a Schengen, US, UK or Irish visa, the whole route is in Turkey visa for Pakistani citizens.

UAE, Indonesia and India: three kinds of 6 months

The UAE government portal is short and clear: "Passports must be valid for at least 6 months from the date of entry into the UAE." Whether you also need a visa depends on your nationality; the same page says GCC citizens do not, some nationalities get a visa on arrival and others must apply in advance.

Indonesia's official e-VOA portal (MOLINA) said, when NeedPro read it on 4 September 2026, that the passport must be valid for at least 6 months from the day you enter, and that a return ticket is one of the required documents. The portal did not open for us on 30 October 2026, so check it again before you travel: molina.imigrasi.go.id.

India counts from a different moment. The official e-Visa page says the passport "should have at least six months validity at the time of making application" and, for an e-Visa, at least two blank pages for stamps. The visa is tied to the passport number you enter, so renew first if you are short of either.

UK: valid for the whole stay, and the ETA follows the passport

GOV.UK says your passport or identity card is checked when you arrive, and "it should be valid for the whole of your stay". There is no fixed buffer on top. The same page warns that some airlines and countries apply stricter rules than the UK border, so ask your airline too.

Many visitors now also need an electronic travel authorisation. GOV.UK says an ETA "lasts for 2 years and is linked to your passport", and if your passport has expired or changed you need a new one. Our article on what a UK ETA costs and who needs one has the fee and the transit rules.

The US: ESTA ends with the passport

The US authorisation works the same way. CBP says ESTA approvals are "typically granted for a two-year period or until the applicant's passport expires, whichever is sooner", and a new passport needs a new ESTA. What limits each stay is a different record, the I-94; see how long an ESTA is valid and what the I-94 date means.

A check before you book

  1. Write down the issue date and expiry date of the passport you will travel on.
  2. Find the destination's rule in the table above and the day it counts from.
  3. Count forward from that day (exit date, entry date, end of stay or application date).
  4. If you are heading to the Schengen area, check that the issue date is less than 10 years before your trip.
  5. If you hold a UK ETA or US ESTA and renew your passport, apply again for the new one.
  6. Ask your airline whether it applies a stricter rule than the border.

For Thailand, the passport is only part of the entry check: see Thailand's 30-day visa exemption and the TDAC arrival card.

Indonesia checks the same thing on arrival: the passport has to be valid for at least six months from the day you enter. More on the Bali entry rules in Bali visa on arrival.

Not every trip needs a passport at all. US citizens flying to Hawaii only need an ID that TSA accepts: see do you need a passport for Hawaii. For Mexico it depends on whether you fly or cross by land: see do I need a passport to go to Mexico.

The rule can depend on your nationality and the type of passport you hold, and the final decision is made at the border. Confirm it on the official site of the destination or with its consulate. Questions people ask

Is the 6 month passport rule the same everywhere?

No. The UAE and Indonesia count 6 months from the day you enter, India counts 6 months from the day you apply for the visa, the Schengen area asks for 3 months after you leave, and the UK asks for validity for the whole stay.

What is the 6 month passport rule for Turkey?

Turkey does not use a flat 6 months. Its migration authority says your passport must be valid at least 60 days longer than the stay you are allowed. For a 90-day stay that means 150 days from entry.

My passport is valid for another year but was issued 11 years ago. Can I enter Schengen?

The EU's Your Europe page says the passport must have been issued within the last 10 years, as well as being valid 3 months after you leave. An 11-year-old passport fails that test even if it has not expired.

Do I need a new UK ETA or US ESTA if I renew my passport?

Yes. GOV.UK says an ETA is linked to your passport and you need a new one if the passport has expired or changed. CBP says the same for ESTA: a new passport needs a new ESTA application.

Can an airline refuse me even if the border would let me in?

GOV.UK notes that some airlines and countries apply stricter passport validity rules than the UK border does. Check with your airline as well as with the destination.
