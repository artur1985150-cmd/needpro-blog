In short

  • US citizens flying from the mainland take a domestic flight. No passport is needed; TSA asks adults 18 and older for an acceptable ID.
  • Since May 7, 2025, a state license only works at TSA if it is REAL ID compliant. A US passport or passport card also works.
  • No acceptable ID at the checkpoint? Since February 1, 2026, TSA ConfirmID costs $45 and TSA will then try to verify who you are.
  • TSA does not require ID from children under 18 traveling within the US. Airlines may have their own rules.
  • Foreign visitors: CBP counts Hawaii as part of the United States, so the normal visa or ESTA rules apply.

No. If you are a US citizen flying from the mainland, Hawaii is a domestic trip and you do not need a passport. Adults need an ID that TSA accepts at the checkpoint, such as a REAL ID compliant driver's license, a US passport or a US passport card. Visitors from other countries are a different case: Hawaii is part of the United States, so they need a US visa or an approved ESTA, exactly as for New York.

Why a flight to Hawaii counts as domestic

Hawaii is a US state, so a flight from Los Angeles to Honolulu never leaves the country. The check you will meet is TSA security screening at your departure airport, and TSA asks for proof of identity, not proof of citizenship: a foreign passport is on its list of accepted IDs.

US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) spells out the same geography for foreign visitors. On November 6, 2026, its ESTA FAQ says the term "United States" refers to "the continental United States, Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, Guam, the Virgin Islands of the United States, and the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands." Whatever rules apply to entering the US apply to Hawaii too.

Which IDs TSA accepts on a flight to Hawaii

TSA warns that the list "is subject to change without notice" and asks travelers to check it again before flying. If you are not sure whether your license is REAL ID compliant, TSA says to ask your state's DMV.

Two details on that page help a lot of people. TSA accepts expired ID "up to two years after expiration" for the forms on its list. And a foreign passport is on the list, so a visitor already in the US can fly to Hawaii with the same passport they entered with.

What if your license is not REAL ID

TSA's REAL ID page says enforcement began May 7, 2025, and the identification page adds that non-compliant licenses "are no longer accepted as valid forms of identification at airports."

You have three ways out:

  1. Fly with your US passport or passport card instead. Both are on TSA's list, and neither has to be REAL ID marked.
  2. Get a REAL ID compliant license from your state DMV before the trip.
  3. Use TSA ConfirmID. TSA says that starting February 1, 2026, a traveler without acceptable ID can pay a $45 fee, and TSA "will then attempt to verify your identity." TSA asks you to pay on its ConfirmID page before heading to the airport.

ConfirmID is an attempt, not a promise. The same TSA page ends with: "If your identity cannot be verified, you will not be allowed to enter the screening checkpoint." For a Hawaii trip with a fixed hotel and flight, carrying a passport is the safer plan.

Do kids need ID to fly to Hawaii?

Not for TSA. Its page says: "TSA does not require children under 18 to provide identification when traveling within the United States." The exception is unaccompanied minors who use TSA PreCheck; they must show an acceptable ID for expedited screening.

Airlines are another matter. TSA tells families to "contact the airline for questions regarding their specific ID requirements for travelers under 18." Ask yours when you book.

Visiting Hawaii from another country: visa or ESTA

Because Hawaii is part of the United States, a foreign tourist needs the same permission as for any US trip. There is no separate Hawaii visa.

If you hold a passport from a Visa Waiver Program country, CBP's program page (checked November 6, 2026) says the program "permits citizens of 42 countries to travel to the United States for business or tourism for stays of up to 90 days without a visa." You must have an approved ESTA before you board. CBP's FAQ adds that children "regardless of age" need their own ESTA, and that "an approved ESTA is not a visa."

ESTA approvals are typically valid for two years or until your passport expires, whichever is sooner; our article on how long an ESTA stays valid covers the details, including why a new passport means a new application. Travelers without an approved ESTA "may be denied boarding," CBP says.

ESTA is only for nationals of Visa Waiver Program countries. If your country is not one of them, you need a US visa. And whichever route you take, CBP officers decide admission when you arrive; ESTA approval "does not determine whether a traveler is admissible."

Your passport also has to be valid for the trip; see passport validity rules by country for how other destinations count it. The agricultural form on flights to Hawaii

What Hawaii does ask about on flights from the mainland is plants and animals. The State's Akamai Arrival site (checked November 6, 2026) says you are "required by state law to fill out this agricultural declaration form for all inbound flights from the continental U.S. to Hawaii."

The Hawaii Department of Agriculture and Biosecurity says the form requires you to declare all plant material, including fruit, vegetables and plant parts, plus animals and microorganisms. "One adult member of a family may complete this declaration for other family members."

The penalties are written on the same page. Failing to declare or giving false information violates Chapter 150A of the Hawaii Revised Statutes and, in certain instances, can mean a maximum penalty of $25,000 and/or up to one year in prison. Some airlines use the digital form only on certain routes, so ask yours which version you will get.

Hawaii cruises and side trips abroad

A cruise that starts and ends at the same US port is called a closed-loop cruise. On its Western Hemisphere Travel Initiative page, checked November 6, 2026, CBP says US citizens on these cruises "are able to enter the United States with a birth certificate and government-issued photo ID."

The same CBP paragraph has a warning: "you may still be required to present a passport to enter the countries your cruise ship is visiting. Check with your cruise line." If your Hawaii itinerary includes a stop in Mexico or another country, that country's rules apply on the day you are there. A checklist before you fly to Hawaii

  1. Look at your driver's license. No REAL ID marking? Pack your passport or passport card.
  2. If your ID expired less than two years ago, TSA still accepts it; older than that, bring something else.
  3. Check your airline's rules for children under 18.
  4. Foreign visitors: confirm you have an approved ESTA or a valid US visa before you book the flight to Honolulu.
  5. On the plane, fill in the agricultural declaration and declare any fruit, plants or animals.
  6. On a cruise with foreign stops, ask the cruise line which documents each port requires.

These are the agencies' rules as they read on November 6, 2026. TSA can change its ID list without notice, and admission of foreign visitors is decided by CBP officers, so check the official pages again close to your travel date.

Questions people ask

Do US citizens need a passport to go to Hawaii?

No. A flight from the mainland to Hawaii is a domestic flight. TSA asks adults for an acceptable ID such as a REAL ID compliant driver's license, a US passport or a US passport card.

Can I fly to Hawaii with a driver's license that is not REAL ID?

TSA says that since May 7, 2025, licenses and state IDs that are not REAL ID compliant are no longer accepted at airports. Bring a passport or another ID from TSA's list, or use TSA ConfirmID for a $45 fee.

Do kids need ID to fly to Hawaii?

TSA does not require children under 18 to show ID when traveling within the United States. TSA suggests contacting the airline about its own ID rules for travelers under 18.

Do I need an ESTA to visit Hawaii?

If you are a citizen of a Visa Waiver Program country and visit for tourism or business for 90 days or less, yes. CBP's ESTA FAQ says 'United States' includes Hawaii. Everyone else needs a US visa.

What is the agricultural form on flights to Hawaii?

The State of Hawaii requires an agricultural declaration on all inbound flights from the continental US. You declare plants, fruit, vegetables and animals. One adult can fill it in for the family.
