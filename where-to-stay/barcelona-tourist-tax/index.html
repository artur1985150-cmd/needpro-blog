In short

  • Barcelona's tourist tax has two parts: a Catalan base rate set by Ley 2/2026, and a city surcharge set by the council.
  • Base rate per person per night from 1 April 2026: 5-star hotel €7.00, 4-star €3.40, tourist flat €4.50, youth hostel €1.00, everything else €2.00.
  • The law caps the city surcharge at €8 per night; the council sets the actual amount by ordinance.
  • Guests aged 16 or under do not pay, and no one pays for more than seven nights per stay.
  • A legal holiday flat in Spain must show a registration number in its listing; no number is a warning sign.

Barcelona's tourist tax in 2026 is a Catalan base rate plus a city surcharge, charged per person per night by the place you stay. Since 1 April 2026 the base rate is €4.50 in a tourist flat, €7.00 in a 5-star hotel and €3.40 in a 4-star. The city adds its surcharge on top, which the law caps at €8 a night. Guests aged 16 or under pay nothing.

Two taxes on one bill

The tax travel sites call "the Barcelona tourist tax" is really two charges.

The first is the Catalan tax on stays in tourist establishments (impuesto sobre las estancias en establecimientos turísticos). Its rates are set by the Catalan parliament. The latest change is Ley 2/2026 of 6 March, published in Spain's official gazette, the BOE, on 23 March 2026. It sets a separate column of rates for the city of Barcelona.

The second is a surcharge (recargo) that only Barcelona may add. Article 34 bis of the same law lets the city council set it by ordinance, up to €8 per night for each type of accommodation. The amount must be the same within each type, except for cruise ships, and it may differ by postcode.

You pay both at once, to the hotel or the flat's host.

Barcelona base rates from 1 April 2026

These are the rates for "Barcelona ciudad" in article 34.1 a) of the law, per person per night. We read them in the BOE on 30 October 2026.

Type of accommodation Base rate
5-star hotel, grand luxury, luxury campsite €7.00
4-star and 4-star superior hotel €3.40
Tourist flat (vivienda de uso turístico) €4.50
Youth hostel €1.00
Other campsites and other accommodation €2.00
Cruise ship, call over 12 hours €4.00
Cruise ship, call of 12 hours or less €6.00

The table in the law has a second column called "tarifa especial" with higher figures (€10.00, €7.00 and €5.00 for some types). The law does not say in that article which stays it covers, so we leave it out of the sums below. If your invoice shows a figure from that column, ask the host which rule they applied.

The city surcharge, and why totals differ online

Published totals do not agree. Some sites say a holiday flat costs €9.50 a night, others €12.50. Both start from the same €4.50 base rate. The first adds a €5 surcharge, the second adds the €8 maximum.

The €8 is a legal ceiling, not necessarily what you are charged. The council's own draft ordinance, posted on its transparency portal in November 2025, set the surcharge at €8 but phased it in: €5 from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027, €6 the following year and €7 the year after. That document is a draft (its approval date is left blank), and we could not find the approved text on an official page. So treat €5 as the planned 2026 figure, and the invoice as the final word.

A rough way to estimate a holiday-flat stay for two adults and five nights, if the €5 surcharge applies: 2 × 5 × (€4.50 + €5) = €95.

Who is exempt, and the seven-night cap

The rules on who pays are in the main Catalan law, Ley 5/2017, which Ley 2/2026 amends. Its consolidated text on the BOE (last updated 10 March 2026) says:

  • stays by people aged 16 or under are exempt (article 27.1 b);
  • stays for health reasons are exempt, and so are stays of the people accompanying the patient (article 27.1 d);
  • stays paid by a public social programme and stays caused by force majeure are also exempt (article 27.1 a and c);
  • no more than seven nights are counted per person (article 33.1).

So a family of two adults and a 12-year-old staying ten nights pays for two people for seven nights each. If you think an exemption covers you, say so at check-in and ask what proof the host needs.

Who collects it and when

The guest owes the tax; the business running the accommodation collects it as "substitute" (sustituto del contribuyente, article 30 of Ley 5/2017). In practice that means the hotel desk or the flat's host. Article 35, in the wording of Ley 2/2026, says the tax must be shown on the invoice they issue to you.

Platforms handle it differently. Some add it to the booking price, some tell you to pay the host on arrival. Read your confirmation for a tourist tax line before you pay again in cash.

Tourist flats: check the registration number

If you book a holiday flat, the tax is the smaller worry. Since 1 July 2025 every short-term rental advertised in Spain must carry a number from the national rental register (Registro Único de Arrendamientos), created by Real Decreto 1312/2024. Spain's housing ministry says platforms must show that number in each listing, and without it the flat cannot legally be marketed. NeedPro checked this rule against the ministry's page on 4 September 2026.

Before you pay for a flat:

  1. Look for the registration number in the listing.
  2. If there is none, ask the host for it in writing.
  3. No number, no booking: it is a sign the flat is not legally on the market.
  4. Ask whether the tourist tax is already in the price.
  5. Pack the passport or ID you booked with.

Your passport at check-in

The last point is not a tax but it happens at the same desk. Spain's Interior Ministry requires every hotel, hostel, campsite and short-term rental to record each guest's identity data. Everyone over 14 signs the entry form (parte de entrada), and the host keeps the register for three years. This applies to flats rented through platforms too. It is a duty on the host, not a fee for you, but you will be asked for the document you booked with.

Questions people ask

How much is the Barcelona tourist tax per night in 2026?

The Catalan base rate since 1 April 2026 is €7.00 in a 5-star hotel, €3.40 in a 4-star, €4.50 in a tourist flat, €1.00 in a youth hostel and €2.00 elsewhere, per person per night. The city surcharge comes on top, capped by law at €8.

How much is the tourist tax for an Airbnb in Barcelona in 2026?

A tourist flat (vivienda de uso turístico) pays the €4.50 base rate plus the city surcharge, per guest per night, for up to seven nights. Check the listing or the invoice for the total; the host must show the tax separately.

Do children pay the tourist tax in Barcelona?

No. The Catalan law exempts stays by people aged 16 or under.

Is there a maximum number of nights?

Yes. The law counts a maximum of seven nights per person, so from the eighth night of the same stay you pay nothing more.

Why do websites give different totals for Barcelona?

They add different city surcharges to the same base rate. The base rate is in the law; the surcharge is set by the city council and can change each year up to the €8 cap.
