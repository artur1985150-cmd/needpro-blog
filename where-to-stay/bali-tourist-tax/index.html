In short

  • Foreign tourists pay a Bali levy of Rp 150,000 per person (Love Bali FAQ, checked 31 October 2026).
  • It is paid once per trip, before you leave Indonesia, and only without cash.
  • Pay online on lovebali.baliprov.go.id or in the Love Bali app before you fly, or at counters at Bali's airport and port.
  • KITAS and KITAP holders, student and family visa holders, crew and diplomats are exempt; golden and some other visa holders must apply at least 5 days before arrival.
  • The levy is separate from the Indonesian visa on arrival and is not refunded once paid.

Yes, Bali has a tourist tax. Foreign tourists pay a levy of Rp 150,000 per person, once per trip, before they leave Indonesia. It is cashless only: pay online on the official Love Bali site or app before you fly, at counters at Bali's airport and port, or at registered hotels and attractions. We checked these rules on the Bali Provincial Government's Love Bali pages on 31 October 2026.

How much is the Bali tourist tax?

The Love Bali FAQ answers in one line: foreign tourists pay Rp 150,000 per person. The exemption list says nothing about age, so if your children travel with you, ask Love Bali before you assume they are free.

The levy is set in Indonesian rupiah. The Love Bali terms say payments are converted in IDR; if you pick another currency on the site, it shows an equivalent at the app's own exchange rate, and your card is still charged the rupiah amount. That is why you see different "USD 10" or "$15" figures online. The rupiah number is the real one.

Who has to pay, and who is exempt?

The FAQ says the levy is owed by "every international tourist who travels to Bali and do tourist activities". Nationality does not matter: an Indian, Australian or British tourist pays the same Rp 150,000. Cruise passengers pay it too.

The exemptions in the FAQ are:

  • holders of diplomatic and official visas;
  • crew members of transport vehicles;
  • holders of a KITAS (temporary stay permit card) or KITAP (permanent stay permit card);
  • holders of family unification visas;
  • holders of student visas;
  • holders of golden visas;
  • holders of other visas.

The first five groups only show their card to the officer. Golden visa holders and "other visas" have to apply for the exemption in the Love Bali system. The terms describe that last group more precisely as visas "outside of tourism purposes" and set a deadline: apply at least 5 days before you reach Bali's arrival gates, or you pay the levy.

A tourist on a visa on arrival or a visa exemption is not on this list. If you are on a business or work visa and not sure whether it counts, ask through the Love Bali contact page before you travel.

When and where to pay

You can pay any time before you leave Indonesia, but the FAQ strongly encourages paying online before you fly, so you skip the queue at arrival. Three ways to pay:

  1. Online, on lovebali.baliprov.go.id or in the Love Bali app. The official app is only offered in the Google Play Store and the Apple App Store.
  2. At payment counters at Bali's airport and port.
  3. During your stay, through endpoints registered in the Love Bali system: hotels, travel agents and tourist attractions.

The site asks for your name as in the passport, email address, passport number, country and arrival date. Someone else can pay for you, for example a family member or a travel agent; cruise agents can pay for whole groups.

Look-alike sites charge for the same voucher. The official address ends in baliprov.go.id. If a page asking for the levy has another address, it is not the provincial government's.

How to pay online, step by step

  1. Open lovebali.baliprov.go.id (or the Love Bali app) and choose the tourist levy.
  2. Enter each traveler's name exactly as in the passport, passport number, country, email and arrival date.
  3. Choose a payment method: Visa, Mastercard, American Express or JCB card, bank transfer, virtual account, UnionPay or QRIS.
  4. Wait for the levy voucher with a QR code; it is sent to the email you entered.
  5. Keep it on your phone. At checkpoints you show the voucher or your passport for scanning.

If the email had a typo and the voucher never arrived, the FAQ says the payment can be verified from your passport data. The site also has a "Resend Voucher" page.

Is the levy part of the Indonesian visa?

No. They are two separate payments to two different authorities. The visa is issued by Indonesia's immigration service; the levy is collected by the Province of Bali. On 31 October 2026 the B1 visa on arrival cost Rp 500,000 on the official e-Visa site, and the Rp 150,000 levy comes on top. Our guide to the Bali visa on arrival covers the visa, the documents and the arrival card.

Visa holders who are exempt from the levy (KITAS, student visa and so on) still need their visa or permit. The exemption is only about the levy.

Is the Bali tourist tax being scrapped or raised?

Search engines suggest "bali tourist tax to be scrapped". We found nothing like that on the official pages. On 31 October 2026 the Love Bali FAQ still listed Rp 150,000 per person, and the government notice page listed the current legal basis: Law No. 15 of 2023 on Bali Province, Bali Regional Regulation No. 2 of 2025 amending Regulation No. 6 of 2023 on the levy, and Governor Regulation No. 25 of 2025 on how it is paid.

Rules can change, so open the FAQ again shortly before your trip.

How Bali compares with tourist taxes elsewhere

Bali's levy is unusual: a flat amount per trip, paid once, not per night. Most European cities do the opposite and charge per person per night through your hotel. In Paris, for example, the rate depends on the hotel's stars. Our page on whether you have to pay tourist tax explains how those systems work, and the Barcelona tourist tax page shows a city where a regional rate and a city surcharge add up.

The practical difference: in Europe the host collects the tax at check-in, while in Bali you are expected to pay it yourself, ideally before you land. Nobody collects it for you unless you book through a registered endpoint. Questions people ask

How much is the Bali tourist tax in 2026?

Rp 150,000 per person, according to the Love Bali FAQ of the Bali Provincial Government, checked on 31 October 2026. The amount is set in rupiah; the dollar figures quoted online are conversions.

When do you pay the Bali tourist tax?

Once per trip, before you leave Indonesia. The official FAQ strongly encourages paying online before you travel to Bali, so you do not queue at the airport.

Where do you pay the Bali tourist tax?

On the official site lovebali.baliprov.go.id or the Love Bali app, at payment counters at Bali's airport and port, or at hotels, travel agents and attractions registered in the Love Bali system.

Can I pay the Bali tourist tax in cash?

No. The FAQ says payment must be cashless: Visa, Mastercard, American Express or JCB cards, bank transfer, virtual account, UnionPay or QRIS.

Do Indian citizens pay the Bali tourist tax?

Yes, if they visit Bali as tourists. The levy applies to every international tourist regardless of nationality; only the visa and permit categories on the exemption list are excused.

Is the Bali tourist tax refundable?

No. The Love Bali terms say levy payments that were successfully received cannot be refunded.
