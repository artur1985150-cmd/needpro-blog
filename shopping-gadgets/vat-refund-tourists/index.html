In short

  • A tourist VAT refund is for goods you carry out of the country, not for hotels, meals or other services.
  • In the EU you must live outside the EU and leave with the goods within 3 months of buying them; customs must see the goods and the refund form first.
  • Minimum spend differs: none on Spain's tax agency page, €50 before VAT in Portugal, AUD 300 from one business in Australia, VND 2,000,000 per shop per day in Vietnam.
  • Australia refunds travellers of any nationality, Australians included; Vietnam pays back 85% of the VAT.
  • Canada has no tourist refund: the CRA says non-resident visitors cannot reclaim GST/HST on purchases.

Tourists can usually reclaim VAT (or GST) on goods they buy and carry home, but each country sets its own minimum, deadline and claim point. On 30 October 2026, the EU required you to live outside the EU and leave with the goods within 3 months; Australia wanted AUD 300 from one business, bought within 60 days of departure; Vietnam paid 85% of the VAT back. Canada refunds nothing to visitors.

What a tourist VAT refund covers

VAT and GST are taxes built into the shop price. A country that runs a tourist refund scheme gives that tax back on goods you export in your luggage, because you will not use them there.

The refund covers goods only. Spain's tax agency excludes services outright. Portugal limits the refund to goods carried in personal luggage, Australia excludes services such as accommodation, and none of the schemes below refunds the tax on hotels or restaurant bills.

The second rule everywhere: customs (or border staff) must see the goods before you leave. Pack purchases you want to claim where you can reach them at the airport.

Spain: the DIVA form and the kiosk

The Agencia Tributaria lets residents of any non-EU country, and residents of the Canary Islands, Ceuta and Melilla, reclaim Spanish VAT. You have at most three months from the purchase to leave the EU with the goods.

How it works:

  1. At the shop, ask for the electronic refund document, the DER.
  2. When you leave the EU, validate the DER at a DIVA kiosk at the airport, port or border.
  3. A green tick means the form is approved and you need do nothing more. A red cross means you go to the customs office.
  4. You get the money through an authorised refund company (cash, card, cheque or bank transfer) or from the shop itself, which the agency says must refund within fifteen days.

Italy and the rest of the EU

The European Commission's Your Europe page sets the common EU rule: you have to prove you live outside the EU and export the goods within 3 months of buying them, presenting the goods and refund documents to customs as you leave. It adds that some EU countries set a minimum purchase value.

Italy is one of the countries with its own minimum. We could not open an official Italian page with the current figure on 30 October 2026, so we do not quote one here. Ask the shop before you pay, or check with Italian customs (Agenzia delle Dogane e dei Monopoli).

Portugal: €50 before VAT

Turismo de Portugal says only private individuals not resident in any EU member state can claim. The minimum is €50 net of VAT. The receipt therefore has to show at least:

  • €61.50 for general goods at 23% VAT;
  • €58 in Madeira and the Azores at 16%;
  • €56.50 for wine at 13%;
  • €53 for goods at 6%, such as books.

Ask the shop for an itemised receipt showing the goods, the amount paid and the amount due back. Show and declare the goods at customs before you leave. The refund can then be paid in cash at major European airports and city centres, or to a credit card or by international cheque.

If you are carrying a lot of cash out of Europe as well, read the EU cash declaration rules first.

Australia: AUD 300 and 60 days

The Australian Taxation Office describes the Tourist Refund Scheme (TRS), run by the Australian Border Force. Unlike the EU schemes it is open to Australian residents too. The conditions:

  • purchases from one business with the same ABN totalling AUD 300 or more including GST, even across several invoices;
  • bought within 60 days before you leave Australia;
  • original tax invoices, in English;
  • the person travelling paid for the goods;
  • the goods go in cabin baggage, unless they are oversized or security rules make the airline check them in;
  • you present the invoices, goods, passport and boarding pass at the TRS facility at least 30 minutes before departure from an airport, or 60 minutes for a cruise.

Crew members of an aircraft or ship cannot claim. If you bring the goods back into Australia later, you must declare them on your incoming passenger card.

Vietnam: 85% back, 60-day invoice

Vietnam's current rule sits in Appendix IV of Decree 181/2025/ND-CP. Foreigners and overseas Vietnamese with a foreign-issued passport get 85% of the VAT back; the bank that pays it keeps 15%. The goods must come from a registered refund shop, be unused and still sealed, and be worth at least VND 2,000,000 per shop per day. The invoice has to be issued no more than 60 days before you leave.

Older news stories quote 30 days. The current decree says 60. The details, including the 30-minute deadline at the airport and the arrival allowances, are in our guide to the VAT refund in Vietnam.

Canada: nothing to claim

The Canada Revenue Agency is direct: a non-resident visitor to Canada cannot claim a rebate of the GST/HST paid on purchases made in Canada. Its only related programme refunds GST/HST to convention sponsors, organisers and exhibitors. Do not count on money back at the airport.

Flying into Great Britain, what you bring back is capped by the UK duty free allowance.

A checklist before you shop

  1. Check that the country has a tourist refund scheme and that you qualify by residence.
  2. Note the minimum spend and whether it counts per receipt, per shop or per day.
  3. Ask the shop for the refund form or tax invoice when you pay.
  4. Keep the goods unused, in their packaging, and reachable in your luggage.
  5. Arrive early enough to see customs before check-in or before boarding, as the scheme requires.
  6. Keep copies of every form until the money arrives.

Other taxes on a trip are not refundable at all. City tourist taxes per night are one example.

Tax authorities, customs and the refund operators decide each claim, and their pages change. Check the current rules on the official site of the country before you travel. Questions people ask

Can tourists get a VAT refund in Europe?

Yes, if you live outside the EU. The European Commission's Your Europe page says you must prove you live outside the EU and take the goods out of the EU within 3 months of buying them, presenting the goods and refund documents to customs as you leave.

Is there a minimum purchase for a VAT refund in Spain?

The Spanish tax agency's general page on traveller refunds, checked on 30 October 2026, did not mention a minimum amount. It sets a time limit instead: you must leave the EU with the goods within three months of the purchase.

What is the minimum for a tax refund in Portugal?

Turismo de Portugal gives €50 before VAT. With the standard 23% rate, that means a receipt of at least €61.50.

Do I get the full VAT back?

Not always. Vietnam refunds 85% of the VAT; the remaining 15% goes to the bank that pays it out. In Spain the refund can come through an authorised refund company or from the shop itself; ask the shop how it pays and whether anything is deducted.

Can I get GST back when I leave Canada?

No. The Canada Revenue Agency says a non-resident visitor cannot claim a rebate of the GST/HST paid on purchases in Canada. Its rebate programme covers convention sponsors, organisers and exhibitors, not shoppers.
