In short

  • Off campus, a study permit allows up to 24 hours a week during regular terms and unlimited hours during a break your school schedules.
  • You can only start working when your study program starts. Arriving early does not let you work early.
  • The program must be full time, post-secondary (or secondary-level vocational in Quebec), at least 6 months long and lead to a degree, diploma or certificate.
  • Students taking only English or French as a second language, only general-interest courses or only prerequisite courses cannot work off campus.
  • You need a Social Insurance Number. For temporary residents it starts with 9 and expires on the same date as the permit.

International students in Canada can work up to 24 hours a week off campus during regular terms, and unlimited hours during a break scheduled by their school. That is IRCC's rule on 30 October 2026. It only applies once your program has started, if you are a full-time student in an eligible program, and you also need a Social Insurance Number.

How many hours a week: the current rule

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) sets the limit on its page about working off campus. During regular school terms or semesters you can work up to 24 hours a week. The limit is for all your off-campus jobs together, not per job.

Most of the pages that rank for this question are news stories from 2024, when the old 20-hour limit was replaced. The 24-hour figure has not changed since, but the conditions around it matter more than the number.

Your study permit may show an older, lower figure, such as 20 hours. IRCC's page says you can still work up to 24 hours a week if you meet all the eligibility requirements.

Breaks: when you can work unlimited hours

The unlimited hours apply only during a break that your designated learning institution (DLI) has scheduled. IRCC gives summer and winter holidays and a reading week as examples.

A gap you create yourself, by taking fewer courses or pausing your studies, is not a scheduled break. If your program has no scheduled breaks at all, the 24-hour limit runs all year, and IRCC warns that going over it breaks the conditions of your permit.

Who can work off campus

IRCC lists every condition, and you need all of them at once:

  • You are a full-time student at a designated learning institution.
  • Your study program has started.
  • The program is post-secondary academic, vocational or professional training, or secondary-level vocational training in Quebec.
  • It is at least 6 months long and leads to a degree, diploma or certificate.
  • You hold a valid study permit, or you applied to extend it before it expired.
  • Your study permit has conditions printed on it that allow off-campus work.
  • You have a Social Insurance Number.

If your permit should allow work but the conditions are missing, IRCC says you can ask for an amendment at no fee.

Part-time students can work only in one case: you are in the last semester, you do not need a full course load to finish, and you were full time until then.

Who cannot work off campus

The same page names the students who are not eligible:

  • your permit says you are not authorized to work off campus;
  • you are only enrolled in English or French as a second language;
  • you are only taking general-interest courses;
  • you are only taking courses you need to be accepted into a full-time program;
  • you are on an authorized leave, or switching schools and not studying at the moment.

If your situation changes and you stop meeting the conditions, you have to stop working.

When you can start working

IRCC is direct: "You can only start working in Canada when you start your study program." If you land a few weeks before term, you still wait for the program start date to take a job.

The same goes for a language course taken before a college program. The language course alone does not give off-campus work rights, so the work starts with the college program.

The Social Insurance Number

To work in Canada you need a Social Insurance Number (SIN), a 9-digit identifier. The Employment and Social Development Canada page is specific about temporary residents: "Your SIN starts with '9' and expires with your immigration documents."

Service Canada's page for temporary residents says you can apply online, by mail or in person at a Service Canada office, with:

  1. a valid study permit, work permit or visitor record from IRCC that allows you to work (an IRCC email is not accepted);
  2. one secondary identity document, such as your passport;
  3. a supporting document if your name is different on your documents.

The same page says you can start working as soon as you apply for the SIN, without waiting for the number. To get the SIN at all, IRCC says your permit has to carry one of the work conditions, for example "May work 24 hours per week off campus or full-time during regular breaks". If your permit says it "does not permit the holder to engage in off-campus employment in Canada", the conditions have to be changed first, and IRCC says that application has a fee.

When your permit is extended, the SIN does not update by itself. Service Canada says you must update your SIN record when you get a new permit with a new expiry date.

After you finish your program

IRCC's page covers two situations where you may keep working without the limit: you applied for a work permit before your study permit expired and are waiting for the decision, or you are starting a new full-time program within 150 calendar days of finishing the previous one. The details depend on your case, so read that part of the page before you plan around it.

If your degree was earned outside Canada and you plan further study or a regulated profession later, recognition is a separate process: see how ENIC-NARIC and foreign degree recognition work.

A check before your first shift

  1. Confirm that your program has started and is at least 6 months long.
  2. Read the conditions printed on your study permit.
  3. Apply for your SIN with the permit and your passport.
  4. Find your school's official calendar of scheduled breaks.
  5. Count your hours across all jobs each week during term.

Travelling during a break is a separate question. A US trip needs its own authorization, so see how long an ESTA is valid, and check your passport validity against your destination's rule.

IRCC decides how your permit conditions apply to you, so confirm on its page or with your school's international student office. Questions people ask

How many hours can an international student work in Canada?

Up to 24 hours a week off campus during regular school terms, across all jobs combined, and unlimited hours during a break scheduled by your school, such as summer and winter holidays or a reading week. This is IRCC's rule as read on 30 October 2026.

My study permit says 20 hours. Can I work 24?

IRCC's page says a permit may show a lower limit such as 20 hours, and that you can work up to 24 hours a week as long as you still meet all the eligibility requirements.

Can I work full time in summer as an international student?

Yes, if summer is a break scheduled by your designated learning institution and you were eligible to work during the term. If your program has no scheduled breaks, the 24-hour weekly limit applies all year.

Does remote work for a company outside Canada count toward the 24 hours?

No. IRCC says remote work for an employer outside Canada does not count toward the 24-hour limit.

Can I work while studying a language course in Canada?

Not off campus if you are only enrolled in an English or French as a second language program. You can only work off campus once you are a full-time student in an eligible post-secondary program that has started.
