In short

  • The EU sets no cap on cash. The rule is a declaration: €10,000 or more, per person, when you enter or leave the EU.
  • The line is '10,000 or more', not 'more than 10,000'. Exactly €10,000 must be declared.
  • Cash includes coins, bearer cheques, traveller's cheques and gold coins or bars of set purity, not only banknotes.
  • Spain fines at least €600 and up to 50% of the amount; France up to 50%. Ireland can seize €1,000 or more if crime is suspected.
  • Travel between EU countries has no EU-wide rule, but France and Spain apply their own national rules.

There is no limit on how much cash you can bring to Europe. The rule is a declaration: if you enter or leave the EU carrying €10,000 or more, or the same value in another currency, you must declare it to customs at that border. Cash here also means coins, bearer cheques and some gold. Rules were checked on customs sites on 30 October 2026.

Is there a limit on cash, or only a declaration?

Only a declaration. The EU's Your Europe page, French customs, Irish Revenue and Spain's tax agency all describe the same thing: you may carry any amount, but at €10,000 or more you fill in a declaration when you cross the EU's outer border.

Most of the confusion online comes from the wording. Several travel-money blogs say "more than €10,000". The official pages say "€10,000 or more". So €10,000 exactly is declared. €9,999 is not.

The threshold is per person, and it counts the total value of everything you carry: euros, dollars, pounds and the rest added together.

What counts as cash?

More than banknotes. The EU list, repeated by each customs service, covers:

  • banknotes and coins, including old currency that banks still exchange;
  • bearer instruments: traveller's cheques, and cheques, promissory notes or money orders signed without a named payee;
  • gold coins with at least 90% gold;
  • gold bars, nuggets or clumps with at least 99.5% gold.

France and Spain add anonymous prepaid cards, the kind not linked to a bank account. A normal debit or credit card is not cash. A bank transfer is not declared either, as Spain's tax agency states directly.

If you are flying out with savings partly in gold coins and partly in dollars, add it all up before you reach the airport.

How much cash can I bring to France?

Any amount. French customs require a declaration for cash of €10,000 or more crossing the French border, and the rule applies to everyone, French resident or not. If you carry money that belongs to someone else, the declaration also names the owner.

You declare online through the DALIA service. The earliest you can file is 30 days before the trip; the latest is the moment you cross the border. A late declaration is not accepted.

France applies the EU regulation to travel to and from non-EU countries and its own Monetary and Financial Code to travel inside the EU. So a train from Brussels to Paris with €12,000 in a bag still needs a declaration. The penalty for not declaring, or declaring falsely, is a fine of up to 50% of the amount, and customs can hold the cash. Read the French customs page before you travel.

How much cash can I bring to Ireland?

Also any amount. If you enter or leave the EU at an Irish airport or port with €10,000 or more, you make a declaration to Customs. Revenue lists the same items as cash: notes, coins, bearer cheques, traveller's cheques, gold coins of 90% purity and gold bars of 99.5%.

Ireland has a second, lower number that has nothing to do with declarations. A Customs officer may search for, seize and keep cash of €1,000 or more when there are reasonable grounds to suspect it comes from crime or is meant for it. A declaration does not switch that power off.

Note one detail for travellers from Britain: Revenue says these rules cover cash moving between Ireland and the UK, but not between Ireland and Northern Ireland. Ask for a stamped copy of your declaration if you want proof you made it.

Spain: forms, fines and the €100,000 rule

Spain counts €10,000 or more per person and per trip, entering or leaving the country. The Agencia Tributaria uses four forms:

  1. E1 for cash you carry to or from a non-EU country.
  2. S1 for cash you carry inside Spain or to and from another EU country.
  3. E2 and S2 for cash you send without travelling with it.

There is also a domestic threshold. Moving €100,000 or more around inside Spain needs a declaration too.

You declare before the movement, not after. Not declaring is a serious offence: the minimum fine is €600 and it can reach 50% of the amount. Customs can provisionally seize the money. Spain's guidance is clear about couples and groups: if €18,000 is one movement of funds, splitting it between two people does not remove the obligation.

Portugal and the rest of the EU

Portugal follows the EU form and the EU definition of cash. If you enter or leave the EU through Lisbon or Porto with €10,000 or more, you declare it to Portuguese customs on the EU cash declaration form. No declaration, or an incomplete one, means penalties.

The EU also covers cash you don't carry. Sending €10,000 or more by post, freight or courier can lead to a disclosure request from customs; the sender or recipient then has 30 days to answer.

For trips between EU countries there are no EU-wide cash rules. The Your Europe page tells travellers to check with customs in the countries of departure, arrival and transit. France and Spain, as shown above, do have national rules. Customs can also act below €10,000 if they suspect a link to crime.

How to declare cash: step by step

  1. Add up everything you carry in every currency, plus cheques, prepaid cards and gold.
  2. If the total is €10,000 or more, find the form of the country where you enter or leave the EU: DALIA online in France, the S1 or E1 form in Spain, the EU cash declaration form elsewhere.
  3. If you have papers that show where the money came from, such as a bank statement or a sale contract, take them with you.
  4. At the airport, go to the red channel or the customs desk. Do not walk through the green channel with a declaration in your pocket.
  5. Ask for a copy of the stamped declaration and keep it for the return trip.

If you are heading further east, the rules change completely. China, for example, caps yuan banknotes and sets a different line for foreign cash: see how much cash you can bring to China.

Related checks before the trip

Cash is one of several things checked at the border. Your passport has to be valid long enough for the Schengen area; the passport validity rules by country list the numbers. If you need a Schengen visa, the consulate also wants travel insurance that meets the Schengen rules. And once you arrive, some cities add a tourist tax per night that is often paid on the spot.

This page sums up what customs publish; it is not legal advice. Customs decide each case. For a large amount, contact the customs service of the country where you cross the border before you travel. Questions people ask

Is there a limit on how much cash I can bring into Europe?

No. The EU does not cap the amount. If you carry €10,000 or more (or the same value in another currency) into or out of the EU, you must declare it to customs. Not declaring is the offence, not the amount.

Do I declare exactly €10,000?

Yes. French, Irish and Spanish customs all use '10,000 euros or more'. €9,999 does not need a declaration; €10,000 does.

Can a couple split €18,000 between them to stay under the limit?

The threshold is per person. Spain's tax agency counts the movement of funds, and its guidance is to declare when in doubt. Splitting one sum to avoid a declaration is the situation customs look for.

Does the rule apply when I fly from Paris to Madrid?

The EU declaration covers the EU's external border only. France and Spain also apply national rules to movements inside the EU, so a declaration can still be required. Check the customs site of each country on your route.

Do I need to declare a bank card or a transfer?

No. Bank transfers are not declared, according to Spain's tax agency. France and Spain do count anonymous prepaid cards not linked to a bank account.
