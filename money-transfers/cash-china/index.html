In short

  • Chinese yuan in cash: RMB 20,000 per person in or out. China Customs says the part above that is prohibited.
  • Foreign cash such as US dollars or euros: no limit on bringing it in, but more than USD 5,000 must be declared in writing.
  • Keep the endorsed declaration: it is what lets you take the same dollars out again.
  • Changing money at a Chinese bank uses a yearly quota of USD 50,000 per person; above it the bank wants documents.

You can bring up to RMB 20,000 in Chinese banknotes into or out of China, and China Customs says anything above that is prohibited. Foreign cash has no upper limit on the way in, but more than USD 5,000, or the same value in other currencies, has to be declared in writing to customs. Both rules come from the China Customs passenger guide, checked on 30 October 2026.

Two different rules: yuan and foreign cash

Travel guides mix these up, so here they are side by side.

Cash you carry What China Customs say Declare? Checked
Chinese yuan (RMB) Up to RMB 20,000 in or out; the part above is prohibited Red channel at RMB 20,000 30 Oct 2026
Foreign currency (USD, EUR, GBP…) No quantitative limit on bringing it in In writing above USD 5,000 equivalent 30 Oct 2026
Traveller's cheques, letters of credit No quantitative limit on bringing them in Not covered by the cash rule 30 Oct 2026

The yuan rule is a ceiling. The dollar rule is a declaration. A declaration does not make RMB 30,000 in notes legal to carry.

How much yuan can I carry?

The customs guide is short on this point: "The limit of RMB allowed to bring in or take out is 20,000 yuan. The exceeding part is prohibited."

That applies in both directions, to Chinese residents and foreigners alike. In practice it matters most on the way out, for example if you withdrew yuan for a long trip and still have a thick envelope on the last day. Spend it, or change it back at a bank before you fly.

The customs table for arriving and departing passengers also lists "RMB20,000 cash or above" among items that go through the red channel. So if you are right at the line, use the red channel and let the officer decide.

How many US dollars can I bring to China?

There is no cap on foreign cash coming in. The customs guide says there are "no quantitative restrictions" on foreign currencies, traveller's cheques or letters of credit brought in, but passengers must "accurately declare" foreign cash worth more than USD 5,000.

The same rule covers euros, pounds, yen or roubles: add up the value of everything in US dollars. If the total passes USD 5,000, you fill in the declaration and use the red channel. The NIA, China's immigration authority, repeats the rule for foreigners: cash above USD 5,000 is declared to customs "in written form".

Flying from the USA adds one more step on your side: the US has its own reporting rule for large sums leaving the country. That is a US rule, published by US Customs and Border Protection, and it sits outside this page.

The NIA notes an exception for people who cross several times on the same day or within a short period. If you do day trips from Hong Kong or Macao into the mainland, ask customs at the crossing how it applies to you, because the official pages don't give the numbers.

Leaving China with cash

When you leave, the red channel covers RMB 20,000 or foreign cash worth USD 5,000 or more.

For foreign cash, the declaration you made on arrival does the work. China Customs release foreign currency on departure "upon the declaration made at the last entry". If you arrived with USD 8,000, declared it, and leave with USD 7,000, the endorsed form shows where the money came from.

Without that record, or if you leave with more than you declared, customs release the difference only against a certificate from the foreign exchange authority or one of its authorised banks. That is why the guide tells passengers who bring in more than USD 5,000 and plan to take it out again to fill in two declaration forms: customs endorse them and hand one back for the departure.

Steps for a trip with a large amount of cash:

  1. Count everything in US dollar value before you land, not in the queue.
  2. Above USD 5,000, take the red channel and fill in the declaration forms in writing.
  3. Keep your endorsed copy with your passport until you leave China.
  4. If you withdraw or buy more foreign cash in China, keep the bank papers: they are your proof on exit.
  5. Before departure, bring yuan down to RMB 20,000 or less.

The USD 50,000 yearly exchange quota

Travel-money pages rarely mention this one. It matters if you plan to live on cash you change in China.

The National Immigration Administration states that each person has an annual quota equal to USD 50,000 for foreign exchange settlement. Inside the quota you change money at a bank with your ID. Above it, the bank wants your ID plus supporting documents. The NIA also tells foreigners to change money only at banks, not with unlicensed people or companies.

The quota is about converting currency at Chinese banks. It is not a customs limit, and it doesn't change what you may carry across the border. But if you arrive with USD 60,000 to cover a long stay, you will hit the bank's paperwork stage before you hit any customs problem.

What if you don't declare?

The customs guide warns that passengers who break the exchange rules can face an administrative fine or have their currency confiscated, and that failing to declare listed items is punishable. It does not give a fixed fine.

This page reports what China Customs and the NIA publish; it is not legal advice. Customs decide at the border. Before a trip with a large sum, contact the Chinese embassy or check the customs guide again, since rules can change.

Other things to sort out before China

Cash is only one of the border checks. Carry a passport with enough validity left; our passport validity table has the numbers by country. Power banks have their own China rules, set out in what power banks you can fly with in China. If you rely on a travel eSIM for payment apps, first check that your phone supports eSIM.

Coming from or through Europe with cash? The EU uses a different system, a €10,000 declaration with no cap at all, explained in how much cash you can bring to Europe. Questions people ask

Is RMB 20,000 a declaration threshold or a limit?

A limit. China Customs states that the amount of RMB allowed in or out is 20,000 yuan and that the exceeding part is prohibited. It is not a line above which you just fill in a form.

How many US dollars can I bring to China from the USA?

China puts no quantitative limit on foreign currency brought in. Above USD 5,000, or the same value in other currencies, you must declare it in writing to Chinese customs on arrival. The US has its own reporting rule when you leave.

Do I need to declare cash when leaving China?

Yes, if you carry RMB 20,000 or foreign cash worth USD 5,000 or more, you use the red channel on exit. Customs release foreign cash on departure against the declaration you made when you last entered.

What happens if I don't declare?

China Customs warn that passengers who break the exchange rules can be fined or have the currency confiscated, and that failing to declare is punishable.

Is there a limit on how much money I can exchange in China?

Each person has an annual quota of USD 50,000 for foreign exchange settlement at banks, according to the National Immigration Administration. Above that you bring your ID and supporting documents to the bank.
