In short

  • Standard travel insurance usually does not cover winter sports; you need a winter sports policy or add-on that names what you will do.
  • GOV.UK warns that many policies exclude off-piste skiing without a guide and damage to hired kit, and many require a helmet.
  • The EHIC gives state healthcare in the EU, EEA and Switzerland on local terms, but not mountain rescue, private clinics or the trip home.
  • Andorra and Monaco do not accept the EHIC; non-EU nationals cannot use one in Switzerland, Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein or Denmark.
  • A Schengen visa needs medical cover of at least €30,000; check separately that the policy covers skiing.

Standard travel insurance usually leaves out winter sports, so for a ski or snowboard trip you need a policy or add-on that names those activities. It should pay for medical treatment, mountain rescue and getting you home, and cover whatever you plan to do, off-piste included. The European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) does not replace it: it gives state healthcare in the EU, EEA and Switzerland, but no rescue, no private clinics and no flight home.

Does ordinary travel insurance cover skiing?

Usually not. The UK government's travel insurance guidance, checked on 6 November 2026, lists "bungee jumping, jet skiing, winter sports or skydiving" as sports that "are not usually included in standard policies". The same page says some activities need specialist insurance or an add-on.

So the first thing to check is not the price. Look in the policy wording for the list of covered activities and find skiing, snowboarding or whatever else you will do. If it is not there, assume you are not covered.

What a winter sports policy should cover

GOV.UK's general list of what travel insurance should include is a good base:

  • treatment in state or private hospitals;
  • emergency transport such as an ambulance, which is often charged separately;
  • medical repatriation, because flying an injured person home can be very expensive;
  • pre-existing medical conditions, declared up front; GOV.UK warns that failing to declare something may invalidate the policy;
  • every activity you will do, since some need specialist cover or an add-on;
  • repatriation costs if you or a family member die abroad.

For a ski trip, add mountain rescue and search and rescue to that list. The EHIC does not pay for them, as the next sections show, so look for them by name in the policy. Ski equipment is the other gap: the FCDO's winter sports page says many policies do not cover damage to rental equipment.

Exclusions that catch skiers out

The FCDO's page on preparing for winter sports, last updated on 1 December 2025, names three conditions that come up again and again:

  • off-piste skiing without a guide is not covered by many policies;
  • damage to hired equipment is often excluded;
  • many policies require you to wear a helmet at all times, whatever the local law says.

Alcohol is the fourth. GOV.UK says most travel insurance does not cover events after excessive drinking or drug use, and the winter sports page adds that your cover may not be valid if you injure yourself or others while intoxicated.

Most policies also exclude conditions you already had, war and terrorism, professional sport and pregnancy past a certain week. In the EU every insurer must publish a short standard summary of each product, the IPID (insurance product information document). Read it before you pay; it is two pages and lists the exclusions plainly.

What the EHIC covers on a ski trip

The EHIC proves you are insured in an EU or EEA country (or Switzerland) and gives you medically necessary state healthcare during a temporary stay, on the same terms as local people. The EU's Your Europe page, checked on 6 November 2026, sets out what it does not do:

  • it does not pay for rescue or repatriation: for transport home after an accident "you will need separate insurance cover";
  • it does not cover private healthcare, only providers in the public system;
  • it does not cover planned treatment abroad.

The same page gives an example that could have been written for this article: a Swedish skier hurt his knee on the second day of a holiday in France, was rescued off the mountain by French rescue services, and had to pay the whole bill at home, because search and rescue is not covered by the EHIC. France's Service-Public site gives the same story with a French skier in Italy.

Citizens Information in Ireland adds two points. In some countries you pay part of the cost, just as local people do, and that share is not refunded. And the EHIC does not cover the cost of flying you home. Its own advice is to consider private travel insurance too, for "extras like winter sports cover".

Where the EHIC does not work at all

Two ski destinations are outside the scheme. Citizens Information says Andorra and Monaco are not part of the EU or EEA, so the EHIC is not accepted there and you should have private health insurance instead.

Nationality matters as well. The EU page says nationals of non-EU countries cannot use their EHIC in Denmark, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway or Switzerland, unless they are refugees living in an EU country or are covered as family members of an EU citizen. A non-EU resident of France with a French EHIC therefore has no EHIC cover in a Swiss resort.

UK residents use the GHIC or a UK-issued EHIC. The FCDO says these work in the EU and Switzerland for state healthcare that cannot wait until you are home, and that they will not cover private treatment, mountain rescue, being flown back to the UK, or lost property.

How to get an EHIC in time

The card is free. The EU page warns against websites that charge to order one; apply to your own health insurer instead.

Timing depends on your country. France's Service-Public says to request the card at least 20 days before departure. If you are late, a provisional certificate can be downloaded straight away and is valid for 3 months. A French EHIC is valid for up to 2 years. In Ireland, Citizens Information says applicants travelling within 10 days can choose an urgent option and get a digital temporary certificate, also valid for 3 months.

Visiting from outside Europe on a Schengen visa

If you need a Schengen visa, the policy has to meet the visa rules first. The German Federal Foreign Office's Schengen visa page, checked on 6 November 2026, asks for travel health insurance with minimum cover of €30,000, valid for the whole Schengen area and the whole stay. EU visa rules also require it to cover medical repatriation, urgent medical care, emergency hospital treatment and death.

That minimum says nothing about sport. A cheap visa policy can meet the €30,000 rule and still exclude skiing. Check both. If you are planning a long winter in the Alps, count your days with our Schengen 90/180 calculator and check your passport against the validity rules. A checklist before you buy

  1. Find your activities by name in the policy: skiing, snowboarding, ski touring, sledging.
  2. Check the off-piste wording: covered at all, only with a guide, only on marked routes?
  3. Look for mountain rescue, search and rescue, and medical repatriation as named benefits.
  4. Check the helmet condition and the alcohol exclusion.
  5. Check cover for your own and hired equipment, and for ski passes and lessons you cannot use after an injury.
  6. Declare every pre-existing condition.
  7. Make sure the policy covers the country: Andorra and Switzerland are outside the EU.
  8. Take your EHIC or GHIC as well, if you are entitled to one; some policies waive the medical excess when you use it, says GOV.UK.

Driving to the resort? Swiss motorways need a vignette, and French cities with low-emission zones need a Crit'Air sticker. Heading for the Christmas markets on the same trip? Their 2026 dates are in our guide.

Questions people ask

Does normal travel insurance cover skiing?

Usually not. GOV.UK lists winter sports among activities that are not usually included in standard policies. You need a winter sports policy or add-on that covers the activities you will actually do.

Is the EHIC enough for a ski holiday?

No. The EU's own page says the EHIC does not cover rescue and repatriation or private healthcare. Its example is a skier who was rescued off a mountain in France and had to pay the whole rescue bill.

Can I use my EHIC in Andorra?

No. Citizens Information (Ireland) says Andorra and Monaco are not in the EU or EEA, so the EHIC is not accepted there and you should have private health insurance.

Does winter sports insurance cover off-piste skiing?

Only if the policy says so. GOV.UK warns that many policies do not cover off-piste skiing without a guide. Read the activities list and the conditions, such as a guide or a helmet.

How much medical cover does a Schengen visa need?

At least €30,000, valid in all Schengen countries for the whole stay, covering medical repatriation, urgent care, emergency hospital treatment and death. That is the visa minimum; it says nothing about skiing.
