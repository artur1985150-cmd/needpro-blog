In short

  • The Crit'Air sticker shows your vehicle's emissions class; you need it to drive in a French low-emission zone (ZFE-m) and during pollution-alert traffic restrictions.
  • It applies to cars registered abroad too; for a foreign plate you can only apply online.
  • Order it only at certificat-air.gouv.fr: €3.85 including postage to a French address; postage abroad is shown at checkout.
  • Driving in a zone without a sticker, or with a banned class, is usually a €68 fixed fine, up to €450.
  • The sticker never expires as long as it stays readable, and it cannot be moved to another windscreen.

The Crit'Air sticker is France's air-quality certificate: a round windscreen sticker that shows how polluting your vehicle is. You need it to drive in a French low-emission zone (ZFE-m) and when a prefect restricts traffic during a pollution episode, and that includes cars on foreign plates. Order it only at certificat-air.gouv.fr. On 6 November 2026 Service-Public gives the price as €3.85 including postage to France; without one in a zone, the usual fine is €68.

What is the Crit'Air sticker?

It sorts vehicles by their emissions of air pollutants. There are six stickers: a green one for electric and hydrogen vehicles, then classes 1 to 5, where a higher number means more pollution. The oldest vehicles get no sticker at all and count as "unclassified".

The class depends on the type of vehicle, the fuel and the Euro emissions standard, or the date of first registration. For cars, Service-Public's table, checked on 6 November 2026, reads:

Do foreign cars need a Crit'Air sticker?

Yes. France's environment ministry says every vehicle is concerned, "registered in France or abroad". Service-Public says the sticker is mandatory to drive in a low-emission zone, and that for a vehicle registered abroad the application is made online only. There is no paper form for foreign plates.

The rule follows the zone, not the trip. A car on Irish, Swiss or Ukrainian plates passing through Lyon needs the sticker exactly as a French car does.

Where do you need it?

In two situations, according to Service-Public:

  • inside a low-emission zone (ZFE-m), where the city decides which classes may enter and when;
  • anywhere the prefect orders differentiated traffic during a pollution episode. This is announced in the media the day before, before 7 pm.

The government's ZFE site says 25 zones are active. Rules differ a lot from city to city. A few big ones, as listed on 6 November 2026:

Several zones tighten their rules in steps. Lyon's central area, for example, adds Crit'Air 2 from 1 January 2028, and Strasbourg plans the same for four of its towns from 2028. Check the city's own page on mieuxrespirerenville.gouv.fr before you drive.

How much does it cost?

Service-Public, checked on 6 November 2026: "€3.85, including postage, for a shipment to France". The environment ministry gives the breakdown: €3.11 plus postage, with postage adjusted for delivery outside France and shown when you order. The money covers the cost of the issuing service; it is not a tax.

The ministry also warns that some websites with no link to it act as intermediaries and sell the sticker "at a price well above the real price". Service-Public is blunter: certificat-air.gouv.fr is the only official site for issuing Crit'Air stickers.

How to order one for a foreign-registered car

  1. Go to certificat-air.gouv.fr and choose the application for a vehicle registered abroad.
  2. Have your vehicle's details to hand: type, fuel, and its Euro standard or date of first registration, the criteria Service-Public lists for the class.
  3. Pay online. The total, including postage to your country, appears before payment.
  4. Order well before the trip, since the sticker comes by post.
  5. When it arrives, stick it on the inside of the windscreen, lower right, visible from outside. On a motorbike or scooter it goes on the fork.

There is one sticker per vehicle. Service-Public says it is issued without a time limit and stays valid for the life of the car as long as it remains readable. It is made so it cannot be moved from one windscreen to another, and it tears if you try to reposition it after sticking.

For questions, the issuing service answers by phone Monday to Friday, 9 am to 5 pm. From outside France the number is +33 800 970 033.

What is the fine?

Service-Public: driving in a ZFE-m with a vehicle that is not allowed, or without a Crit'Air sticker, "is sanctioned by a fine up to €450. In general, it is a flat-rate fine from €68." The same applies if you ignore pollution-alert restrictions. The vehicle can also be immobilised and impounded.

The environment ministry adds detail. A light vehicle gets a third-class fine (€68 standard), a heavy vehicle a fourth-class one (€135). Parking in an active zone without a sticker, if your vehicle is eligible for one, is also an offence.

Who decides, and what about exemptions?

Each city sets its zone, hours and exemptions, and Service-Public notes that a zone cannot be closed to vehicles showing a disabled parking card. If you have a classic car, Service-Public says to ask the local council what applies. We are not the authority: when in doubt, read the zone's own page or contact the town hall before you drive in.

Questions people ask

Do UK and other foreign cars need a Crit'Air sticker?

Yes, to drive in a French low-emission zone or during pollution-alert restrictions. France's environment ministry says the certificate covers vehicles registered in France or abroad, and Service-Public says foreign-registered vehicles apply online only.

How much does a Crit'Air sticker cost?

Service-Public gives €3.85 including postage for delivery in France. The ministry breaks it down as €3.11 plus postage, and says postage is adjusted for delivery outside France and shown when you order.

Where do I buy a Crit'Air sticker?

Only at certificat-air.gouv.fr. Service-Public calls it the only official site; the ministry warns that other sites act as middlemen and charge much more.

What is the fine for no Crit'Air sticker?

Service-Public says up to €450, usually a fixed fine of €68 for a car. The ministry gives €135 for heavy vehicles. The vehicle can also be immobilised and impounded.

Does the Crit'Air sticker expire?

No. It is issued without a time limit and stays valid as long as it is readable. It is made so it cannot be moved from one windscreen to another.
