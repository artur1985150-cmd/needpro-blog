Does Netflix work in other countries?

It does, and you don't need to change any settings. Netflix's help page on using the service outside your home says you can keep watching on your phone, tablet and computer as usual, or sign in on a new TV in a hotel or a holiday rental.

The catch is the catalog. Netflix's Terms of Use say you may watch "primarily within the country in which you have established your account", and only where Netflix offers the service and has licensed the content. In practice, when you open the app in another country, you see what is licensed there.

Downloads deserve a closer look. The Terms of Use say an offline title stops playing if you go online in a country where you couldn't stream it. So downloading at home doesn't guarantee the episode will play once you land.

Travelling inside the EU: the portability rule

If you live in an EU country and pay for an online content service, EU law gives you a right that most travel guides skip. The European Commission's Your Europe page puts it this way: "You have the right to access your paid-for online content services in the same way as you would in your home country."

While you are temporarily in another EU country, the provider:

  • can't charge you extra for access abroad;
  • can't limit access because you are abroad;
  • must give you the same content, the same number and range of devices, the same number of users and the same functions;
  • can't deliberately lower the quality of the content (your Wi-Fi or mobile signal can still do that).

This covers subscriptions for TV, films, music, e-books, games and sports events. Netflix says the same on its help pages for EU countries: for countries within the EU it provides "the same content as your home country for a limited period of time."

Streaming over mobile data comes out of your phone plan's data allowance, under the EU roaming rules. If you rely on a travel eSIM instead, read our guide on whether your phone supports eSIM before the trip.

How long is "temporary", and how do they know where you live?

The Your Europe page doesn't give a number of days. Its example is a student on a three-month Erasmus placement who kept her residence in Belgium: her provider had to restore access abroad.

To decide which EU country you live in, the provider may use up to two checks from a fixed list: an ID card or electronic ID, payment details, the address of a set-top box, payment of a public broadcasting licence fee, an internet or phone contract, local electoral roll registration, local tax payment, or a utility bill. Your billing or postal address, your own statement of address, or your IP address count only alongside one of those.

Free services are a different case. Access to free subscriptions abroad is optional, so whether a free account travels with you is up to the provider.

What the EU rule doesn't cover

There are three limits.

  1. Trips outside the EU. The rule protects EU residents travelling within the EU. Netflix's own page says that if you travel to a country outside the EU, your choices may be different.
  2. Moving. The right is for temporary presence. Once you relocate, you subscribe in the new country.
  3. The geo-blocking rules. The EU also has a Geo-blocking Regulation that stops traders treating customers differently by nationality or residence. According to the European Commission, copyright-protected content such as streaming, e-books, music and online games was outside its full scope at the time of the Commission's review. So for Netflix, the portability rule is the one that matters.

If you think a provider breaks the portability rule, Your Europe points to its advice service on EU rights. Ask there or check your country's consumer body; we can't tell you how a particular complaint will end.

What about using a VPN?

This page doesn't explain how to use one, and here is why. Netflix's help page says it may show the error "You seem to be using a VPN or proxy" when a VPN makes your device look like it is somewhere else. VPNs aren't supported for Live events or on the ad-supported plan. And the Terms of Use tie viewing to the country where you set up the account.

If you see that error and you aren't using a VPN, Netflix suggests running the test at fast.com: if the country under "Client" doesn't match where you are, something on the network, sometimes antivirus software, is routing your connection elsewhere.

A second home or regular trips to the same place

Netflix has a specific step for people who split time between two places. Once a month, from the main place where you watch, connect to the internet, open Netflix on a phone or go to Netflix.com on a computer, and stream a show for a few seconds. Do the same when you arrive at the second place. Netflix says this lets you keep watching without interruption.

Moving to another country: what happens to your account

Here the EU rule stops helping, and Netflix is direct about it. Its moving page says: "If you're moving to a new country, you will need to cancel your membership and sign up again in the new country." You cancel, wait for the membership to end, and restart while you are physically in the new country.

Until you do, you keep paying the old country's price in its currency. After the restart, the price, billing currency, payment methods, catalog, and audio and subtitle options all follow the new country. Maturity ratings differ too, so set parental controls again.

Your history isn't lost at once. Netflix keeps viewing activity, recommendations, ratings and account details for 24 months after an account closes.

When you sign up for any new streaming service in an EU country, you normally have 14 days to cancel a contract made online. For streamed content that right ends once streaming has started and you expressly agreed to lose it, so read the checkbox at signup.

Netflix isn't the only account tied to a country. Your iPhone's Apple Account has its own conditions for moving, such as spending the balance and ending subscriptions first: see how to change your App Store country.

Before a trip or a move: a short checklist

  1. Short EU trip with a paid plan: nothing to change. Your home catalog should follow you.
  2. Trip outside the EU: expect a different catalog, and don't count on downloads playing.
  3. Paying by gift card: top up or add a backup card before you leave.
  4. Moving: cancel near the end of your billing period, then sign up again once you are in the new country.
  5. Flying within Europe for the trip or the move: our page on EU261 compensation covers delayed and canceled flights.

These are Netflix's and the EU's published rules, not a promise of what a given app will show you; the provider decides that, and the consumer body or a court decides disputes. Questions people ask

Can I use my Netflix account in another country?

Yes. Netflix says you can watch on your phone or computer as usual while traveling, or sign in on a hotel TV. What you can watch depends on the country you're in, and some downloads may stop playing there.

Do I get my home Netflix catalog in another EU country?

For a paid plan, yes, while your stay is temporary. The EU portability rule requires the same content, devices, number of users and functions as at home, with no extra charge. Netflix's help page for EU countries says it provides the same content as your home country for a limited period.

What happens to my Netflix account if I move abroad?

It doesn't move with you. Netflix's help page says to cancel your membership, wait for it to end, and sign up again while you are in the new country. Price, currency, payment methods and the catalog change.

Will I lose my viewing history if I cancel and sign up again?

Not straight away. Netflix keeps viewing activity, recommendations, ratings and account details for 24 months after an account closes, so you can restart and pick up where you left off.
